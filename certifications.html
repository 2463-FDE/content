Cloud paths & certifications

Pick one cloud and follow it. Your chosen cloud is your primary implementation plane; the other cloud appears as the comparison substrate in optional depth. Shared concepts are written once for both paths, and every lesson's required path fits inside Core 30 on whichever cloud you choose. Both shows everything and runs longer.

How the paths work

  • One cloud is the expected route. Lessons with provider content show your cloud's block open and the other cloud's block collapsed as an optional comparison. Nothing is removed.
  • Core 30 holds per path. Each lesson publishes one pacing label per cloud, measured on that cloud's required path: reading, one or two interactions, and the check.
  • Links keep the path. Add ?cloud=aws, ?cloud=gcp or ?cloud=both to any lesson link to share a view; it applies to that browser tab and does not change the viewer's saved choice.
  • Works without JavaScript. Every provider block is open and labelled AWS or GCP, and both pacing labels are visible.
  • No cloud accounts are used. Provider labs in this curriculum are in-page simulations and read-only code — nothing is provisioned, billed, or deployed.

Week 6 handover: negotiate the team-primary cloud

A required team exercise at the Project 2 kickoff. It practises the same skills the curriculum assesses: an architecture decision recorded with lock-vs-defer reasoning, requirements read from the client's request cards (the client's written Project 2 asks — requested feature, constraints, and acceptance criteria), and a position defended with evidence rather than preference.

  1. Bring your evidence. Each member states their personal view and what their Project 1 build ran on, with one number — an eval score, a latency, or a cost note from their own work.
  2. Read the client constraints. Separate hard constraints in the request cards from negotiable preferences; a stated client constraint outranks anyone's familiarity.
  3. Compare both options. Fill the options table for AWS and GCP as the team primary, including the cost of re-platforming each member's Project 1 work.
  4. Decide one primary. One cloud for the team implementation, shared repository, deployment examples, and required Core-30 work. No split primary architecture; the other provider stays as comparison.
  5. Record it. Commit a concise ADR using the team-primary cloud ADR template, including the negotiation record, and cite it in the team's first readout.

Curriculum ties: ADRs and lock-vs-defer decisions (Week 5 · Day 2), requirements synthesis (Week 1 · Day 3 (Part 2)), and the Project 2 handover that inherits Project 1's decisions. Individual learners keep their own curriculum view; only the team's build follows the recorded cloud.

Aligned to Google's AI Professional Certificate. Each of its courses lists the live lessons that teach the same theme.

What this certificate is. A Google career certificate for using AI at work — "no previous experience is required" — taught through Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Google Workspace, and Google AI Studio, with 20+ hands-on activities and graded assessments. It is not an exam and publishes no domains or weights. This program covers the overlapping themes at engineering depth; three of its courses (content creation; data analysis in Sheets; app deployment) have no lesson here.
