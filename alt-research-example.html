What a strong Alt Research submission looks like

A worked example. This isn't one of your assigned weeks — it's here so you can see the shape and the depth we expect before you write your own. Read it as a template, not as content to memorize.

The job (Karsun-style): given a real client constraint, name the option you'd recommend, compare it honestly against 2–3 alternatives with a maturity read, own the anti-patterns you'd have to defend, and cite every current-state claim. Then defend it live for ~10 minutes — your group will probe it. Picking the trendiest tool isn't the win; defending a recommendation against the constraint is.
Step 1 · The constraint (in the client's words)
"Our customer-facing loan portal is a six-year-old server-rendered app nobody wants to touch. Leadership wants a modern front end — something we can actually hire for, that renders fast and ranks on Google for our public loan-product pages, and that won't take the team a year to learn. Pick a framework and tell me why." — Dana, VP of Lending Ops

Reframe (the FDE move): the real requirements hiding in that ask are (a) server-side rendering / SEO for public pages, (b) a US hiring pool the client can staff from, (c) a gentle ramp for an existing team, and (d) long-term maintainability. Name them explicitly — the client didn't.

Step 2 · The candidates

mature Next.js (React) — the programme default for this client.
solid Nuxt (Vue) — progressive, gentle learning curve.
solid Angular — batteries-included, opinionated, enterprise-leaning.

Maturity reads are claims you must back. Cite the framework's own release/LTS page + a current ecosystem/usage source, dated, in Step 5 — don't assert "most popular" from memory.

Step 3 · Honest comparison against the constraint
Criterion (from the constraint)Next.js / ReactNuxt / VueAngular
SSR / SEO for public pagesFirst-class (App Router, RSC, streaming)First-class (Nuxt)SSR via Angular Universal; heavier setup
US hiring poolLargest (React)SmallerMid; strong in enterprise/regulated shops
Learning ramp for the teamModerate (RSC/server-vs-client boundary is the trap)GentlestSteepest (DI, RxJS, modules)
Structure / guardrails for a big teamConvention, not enforcedConvention, not enforcedOpinionated, enforced
Long-term maintainabilityStrong, fast-moving (pin versions)Strong, stableStrong, very stable

No clean sweep — and that's the point. Each wins something. Your job is to weight the criteria for this client and defend the weighting.

Step 4 · Recommendation + one-paragraph rationale

Recommend: Next.js. The two non-negotiables in Dana's ask are SSR/SEO for public loan pages and a hiring pool she can staff from — Next.js wins both decisively, and it's mature enough to inherit safely. Angular's enforced structure is tempting for a regulated lender, but the steeper ramp fights requirement (c) and the smaller hiring pool fights (b); I'd revisit Angular only if the team were already Angular-shops. Vue/Nuxt is the closest runner-up and the right pick if "gentle ramp" outranked "hiring pool" — but for Meridian it doesn't.

Step 5 · Anti-patterns I'd have to own on defense
  • Next.js: the App Router server/client-component boundary is where teams shoot themselves — over-adopting server components before the team understands it leaks data-fetching bugs. Mitigation: start with the boundary documented + a lint rule.
  • Don't chase the bleeding edge: pin the major version; Next moves fast and "latest" in a tutorial may differ from your LTS. (This is exactly the kind of current-state claim you must cite, not assume.)
  • SEO isn't free: SSR has to be wired correctly (metadata, caching) or you get the worst of both worlds — own the work, don't assume the framework does it.

These are the probe questions your group will ask on Friday. A strong defender names the anti-patterns before they're asked.

Step 6 · Sources (every current-state claim, dated)

This section is required. Each maturity/popularity/version claim above ties to a source you retrieved via /web-research, with the date you pulled it (the landscape moves — an undated claim is a guess). Example shape:

  • Next.js — official releases / versioning page · retrieved YYYY-MM-DD
  • State-of-JS or framework usage survey (hiring-pool signal) · retrieved YYYY-MM-DD
  • Vue / Nuxt docs (SSR support) · retrieved YYYY-MM-DD
  • Angular docs (Universal SSR, LTS schedule) · retrieved YYYY-MM-DD
What good looks like (the bar)
  • Reframed the ask — surfaced the requirements the client didn't state.
  • Named a recommendation and one runner-up, not a wishy-washy "it depends."
  • Compared honestly — every candidate wins something; no strawmen.
  • Weighted to the client — the rationale ties to this constraint, not generic pros/cons.
  • Owned the anti-patterns before being asked.
  • Cited every current-state claim, dated, via /web-research.
  • Defends in ~10 min and survives the group's probe questions.
