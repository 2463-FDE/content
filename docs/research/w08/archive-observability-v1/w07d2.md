## Claims

### Offline vs online: the split is about the *target*, not the metric

- Offline evaluations run on **dataset examples** (input + reference output) and are for "**Benchmarking**… **Regression testing**: ensure new versions don't degrade quality… **Unit testing**… **Backtesting**." Online evaluations run on **runs and threads from production tracing** and are for "**Real-time monitoring**… **Anomaly detection**… **Production feedback**." — [Evaluation concepts — LangSmith](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/evaluation-concepts)
- The consequence, stated directly: "This difference in targets determines what you can evaluate: **offline evaluations can check correctness against expected answers, while online evaluations focus on quality patterns, safety, and real-world behavior.**" Production runs "do not include reference outputs. Online evaluators must assess quality without knowing what the 'correct' answer should be." — [Evaluation concepts — LangSmith](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/evaluation-concepts)
- Therefore only **reference-free** evaluators survive the jump to production: "Safety checks: toxicity detection, PII detection, content policy violations; Format validation: JSON structure, required fields, schema compliance; Quality heuristics…; Reference-free LLM-as-judge: clarity, coherence, helpfulness, tone." Reference-**based** evaluators (correctness, factual accuracy vs ground truth, exact match) "only work for offline evaluation." — [Evaluation concepts — LangSmith](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/evaluation-concepts)
- They are a **loop**, not a choice: "Online evaluations surface issues that become offline test cases, offline evaluations validate fixes, and online evaluations confirm production improvements." — [Evaluation concepts — LangSmith](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/evaluation-concepts)
- Google names the same bridge as a first-class dataset source: you can "**Sample directly from production logs** to evaluate the real-world usage of your model." — [Gen AI evaluation service overview — Vertex AI](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/models/evaluation-overview)
- OpenAI's five-step workflow ends on the same point: "**Continuously evaluate.** Set up continuous evaluation (CE) to run evals on every change, monitor your app to identify new cases of nondeterminism, and **grow the eval set over time**." — [Evaluation best practices — OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices)

### Online eval is a cost and privacy decision, not just a quality one

- **Filter, then sample.** LangSmith online evaluators take a run filter (e.g. "runs where a user left feedback indicating the response was unsatisfactory," runs invoking a specific tool call, runs matching metadata like `plan_type`) plus a sampling rate: "to control costs, you may want to set a filter to only apply the evaluator to 10% of traces… you would set the sampling rate to 0.1." — [Set up LLM-as-a-judge online evaluators — LangSmith](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/online-evaluations)
- **Hidden billing side-effect worth teaching:** "When an online evaluator runs on any run within a trace, the trace will be **auto-upgraded to extended data retention**. This upgrade will impact trace pricing." — [Set up LLM-as-a-judge online evaluators — LangSmith](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/online-evaluations)
- Microsoft Foundry's continuous evaluation "continuously evaluates agent interactions at a **set sampling rate**"; you configure `AgentEvaluationSamplingConfiguration` with `samplingPercent` (0-100) and `maxRequestRate` per hour "within the **system limit of 1000/hour**," with results emitted to Application Insights as `gen_ai.evaluation.result` traces. — [Continuously evaluate your AI agents (preview) — Microsoft Foundry](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/how-to/continuous-evaluation-agents)
- **Privacy hook (matters for both client projects):** Foundry's judge reasoning is redacted by default; you must set `redact_score_properties=False` to see it, and the doc warns "**Reasoning explanations might mention sensitive information based on the content of the conversation.**" An online evaluator writes model-generated commentary about production payloads into your telemetry store. — [Continuously evaluate your AI agents (preview) — Microsoft Foundry](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/how-to/continuous-evaluation-agents)

### LLM-as-a-judge works — at roughly human noise levels

- The primary result: "strong LLM judges like GPT-4 can match both controlled and crowdsourced human preferences well, achieving **over 80% agreement, the same level of agreement between humans**. Hence, LLM-as-a-judge is a scalable and explainable way to approximate human preferences, which are otherwise very expensive to obtain." — [Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena — arXiv 2306.05685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)
- The same paper is the canonical source for the bias list: "We examine the usage and limitations of LLM-as-a-judge, including **position, verbosity, and self-enhancement biases**, as well as limited reasoning ability." — [arXiv 2306.05685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)
- Judges are not a monolith. CALM identifies and automatically quantifies **12 key potential biases**, concluding that "while advanced models have achieved commendable overall performance, **significant biases persist in certain specific tasks**" — i.e. a frontier judge does not retire the problem, it relocates it. — [Justice or Prejudice? Quantifying Biases in LLM-as-a-Judge — arXiv 2410.02736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02736)

### Position bias: big enough to invert a verdict

- "The quality ranking of candidate responses **can be easily hacked by simply altering their order of appearance in the context**. This manipulation allows us to skew the evaluation result… e.g., **Vicuna-13B could beat ChatGPT on 66 over 80 tested queries** with ChatGPT as an evaluator." — [Large Language Models are not Fair Evaluators — arXiv 2305.17926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926)
- Documented mitigations, in the authors' own order: (1) **Multiple Evidence Calibration** — "requires the evaluator model to generate multiple evaluation evidence before assigning ratings"; (2) **Balanced Position Calibration** — "aggregates results across various orders to determine the final score"; (3) **Human-in-the-Loop Calibration** — "introduces a balanced position diversity entropy to measure the difficulty of each example and **seeks human assistance when needed**." — [arXiv 2305.17926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926)
- **[INSTRUCTOR COMMENTARY — not sourced; authorial inference. Do not attribute to Wang et al. or any vendor.]** (3) is the cheapest real-world win — use judge disagreement across orderings as a *routing signal* to the human expert queue. The paper supplies the entropy measure and the "seeks human assistance when needed" framing; the queue-routing framing is ours.

### Verbosity bias: a confounder you can regress out

- "Even simple, known confounders such as **preference for longer outputs** remain in existing automated evaluation metrics… AlpacaEval **is known to favor models that generate longer outputs**." — [Length-Controlled AlpacaEval — arXiv 2404.04475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475)
- The fix is a counterfactual: "What would the preference be if the model's and baseline's output had the **same length**?" — fit a GLM on the mediator (length difference), then predict preference conditioned on zero length difference. Effect: "Length-controlling not only improves the robustness of the metric to manipulations in model verbosity, but we also find that it **increases the Spearman correlation with LMSYS Chatbot Arena from 0.94 to 0.98**." — [arXiv 2404.04475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475)
- **[INSTRUCTOR COMMENTARY — not sourced; authorial inference. Do not attribute to Dubois et al.]** Practical rule for students: when an eval score jumps after a prompt change, check output length before celebrating. (The paper establishes that length is a live confounder and that it can be regressed out; it does not state this as a workflow rule.)

### Self-preference bias: a measured mechanism, not folklore

- "**self-preference**, where an LLM evaluator scores its own outputs higher than others' while human annotators consider them of equal quality." — [LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations — arXiv 2404.13076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076)
- Why: "out of the box, LLMs such as GPT-4 and Llama 2 have **non-trivial accuracy at distinguishing themselves** from other LLMs and humans. By fine-tuning LLMs, we discover a **linear correlation between self-recognition capability and the strength of self-preference bias**; using controlled experiments, we show that the **causal** explanation resists straightforward confounders." — [arXiv 2404.13076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076)
- **[INSTRUCTOR COMMENTARY — not sourced; authorial inference. Do not attribute to Panickssery et al. or any vendor.]** Operational rule: the judge model must differ from the generator model, and "we A/B'd two prompts on the same model and let that model pick" is a broken experiment. (The paper demonstrates the mechanism and its causal status; the "different model or the experiment is broken" rule is our stated consequence, not their recommendation.)

### Mitigations the vendors actually prescribe

- **Force reasoning, then throw it away:** "Ask the LLM to think first before deciding an evaluation score, and then **discard the reasoning**. This increases evaluation performance, particularly for tasks requiring complex judgement." — [Define success criteria and build evaluations — Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/develop-tests), § *Tips for LLM-based grading* → "Encourage reasoning"
- **Constrain the output space:** "instruct the LLM to output only 'correct' or 'incorrect', or to judge from a scale of 1-5. Purely qualitative evaluations are hard to assess quickly and at scale," and "have detailed, clear rubrics." — [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/develop-tests), § *Tips for LLM-based grading* → "Empirical or specific" / "Have detailed, clear rubrics"
- **Prefer comparison to absolute scoring:** "**LLMs are better at discriminating between options.** Therefore, evaluations should focus on tasks like pairwise comparisons, classification, or scoring against specific criteria instead of open-ended generation." — [Evaluation best practices — OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices)
- LangSmith agrees on shape: pairwise "works well when directly scoring an output is difficult but comparing two outputs is straightforward. For example, in summarization tasks, **choosing the more informative of two summaries is often easier than assigning an absolute score to a single summary**." — [Evaluation concepts — LangSmith](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/evaluation-concepts)
- **Use the cheapest grader that works.** Anthropic's ladder: code-based grading is "fastest and most reliable, extremely scalable"; human grading is "most flexible and high quality, but slow and expensive. **Avoid if possible**"; LLM-based grading is "fast and flexible… **Test to ensure reliability first then scale.**" — [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/develop-tests), § *Grade your evaluations* ("choose the fastest, most reliable, most scalable method")
- **Few-shot the judge:** "LLM-as-judge evaluators require careful review of scores and prompt tuning. **Few-shot evaluators**, which include examples of inputs, outputs, and expected grades in the grader prompt, often improve performance." — [Evaluation concepts — LangSmith](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/evaluation-concepts)
- Google's current default is per-prompt **adaptive rubrics**: "Generates a unique set of pass or fail rubrics for each individual prompt… **Rubrics are dynamically generated for each prompt, like unit tests**," which they position as more objective and explainable than a single 1-5 static score. — [Gen AI evaluation service overview — Vertex AI](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/models/evaluation-overview)

### Building the gold set WITH the client

- **Only domain experts can produce the labels — and they disagree with each other.** MedHELM built "a **clinician-validated taxonomy spanning 5 categories, 22 subcategories, and 121 tasks developed with 29 clinicians**," because near-perfect medical-licensing-exam scores "inadequately reflect the complexity and diversity of real-world clinical practice." — [MedHELM — arXiv 2505.23802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23802)
- **The number to put on a slide:** "Our LLM-jury evaluation method achieved good agreement with clinician ratings (**ICC = 0.47**), **surpassing both average clinician-clinician agreement (ICC = 0.43)** and automated baselines including ROUGE-L (0.36) and BERTScore-F1 (0.44)." Two lessons: measure inter-annotator agreement because experts are noisy, and the judge's realistic target is *inter-expert agreement*, not perfection. — [arXiv 2505.23802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23802)
- **Criteria drift — why you cannot spec the rubric in a kickoff meeting:** "we identify a phenomenon we dub **criteria drift**: **users need criteria to grade outputs, but grading outputs helps users define criteria**. What is more, some criteria appear **dependent** on the specific LLM outputs observed (rather than independent criteria that can be defined *a priori*), raising serious questions for approaches that assume the independence of evaluation from observation of model outputs." — [Who Validates the Validators? — arXiv 2404.12272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12272)
- EvalGen's whole premise is the FDE stance: LLM-generated evaluators "simply inherit all the problems of the LLMs they evaluate, requiring further human validation"; the interface "asks humans to grade a subset of LLM outputs; this feedback is used to **select implementations that better align with user grades**." Alignment is "**subjective and iterative**." — [arXiv 2404.12272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12272)
- **Start small, hand-curated:** "**Start with manually curated examples.** Create 5-10 examples of what 'good' looks like for each critical component. These examples serve as your ground truth"; grow to "**10-20 high-quality examples** covering common scenarios and edge cases," then extend with historical traces (negative user feedback, long latency, errors) and synthetic expansion. — [Evaluation concepts — LangSmith](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/evaluation-concepts)
- **Then favor volume:** "**Prioritize volume over quality:** More questions with slightly lower signal automated grading is better than fewer questions with high-quality human hand-graded evals," and "**Be task-specific:** design evals that mirror your real-world task distribution. Don't forget to factor in edge cases!" — [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/develop-tests)
- **Named anti-patterns:** "**Biased design**: creating eval datasets that don't faithfully reproduce production traffic patterns"; "**Vibe-based evals**: using 'it seems like it's working' as an evaluation strategy, or waiting until you ship before implementing any evals"; "**Ignoring human feedback**: not calibrating your automated metrics against human evals." Its matching tip: "**Maintain agreement:** use human feedback to calibrate automated scoring." — [Evaluation best practices — OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices)
- OpenAI's worked example for summarization explicitly names the client-expert step: "Use a mix of production data… and **datasets created by domain experts (writers) to determine a 'good' summary**," and later "Ensure your test data includes typical cases, edge cases, and adversarial cases. **Use human expert labellers.**" — [Evaluation best practices — OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices)
- **Annotation workflow mechanics** (vendor-agnostic template): annotation queues "streamline structured collection of human feedback on runs… providing organized workflows with **prescribed rubrics**, team collaboration features, and progress tracking," support "**configuring multiple reviewers per run**, enabling reservations to prevent conflicts, and **exporting annotated runs directly to datasets**." Single-run queues also support "**assertions**, free-form acceptance criteria that an offline evaluator can grade future runs against" — this is how one clinician objection becomes a permanent test. Pairwise queues exist for fast A/B. — [Evaluation concepts — LangSmith](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/evaluation-concepts) · [Use annotation queues — LangSmith](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/annotation-queues)
- **Version the dataset or the gate is meaningless:** LangSmith "automatically creates dataset versions when examples change… **Target specific versions in CI pipelines to ensure dataset updates don't break workflows.**" Splits let you separate exploratory examples from the main evaluation set ("staged rollout: keep exploratory examples isolated until you're ready to include them"). — [Evaluation concepts — LangSmith](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/evaluation-concepts)

### Regression gates on eval score

- **Why the gate exists:** "Generative AI is variable. Models sometimes produce different output from the same input, which makes **traditional software testing methods insufficient** for AI architectures. Evaluations (evals) are a way to test your AI system despite this variability." Also: "**Adopt eval-driven development:** evaluate early and often. Write scoped tests at every stage." — [Evaluation best practices — OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices)
- **Success criteria must be numeric to be gateable.** Anthropic's example of turning a hazy criterion into a threshold: bad = "Safe outputs"; good = "**Less than 0.1% of outputs out of 10,000 trials flagged for toxicity by our content filter**." Criteria should be "Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant." — [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/develop-tests)
- OpenAI's worked thresholds look exactly like acceptance criteria: "On a held-out set of 1000 reference transcripts → summaries, the implementation should achieve a **ROUGE-L score of at least 0.40 and coherence score of at least 80%**"; for Q&A over docs, "**Context recall of at least 0.85, context precision of over 0.7, and 70+% positively rated answers**." — [Evaluation best practices — OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices)
- **Gate shape A — assertions in your existing test runner.** The LangSmith pytest plugin is for when "you want to **assert binary expectations**: track assertions in LangSmith and raise assertion errors locally (**e.g. in CI pipelines**)." Mark tests `@pytest.mark.langsmith`, `t.log_inputs` / `t.log_reference_outputs` / `t.log_outputs` / `t.log_feedback`, then plain `assert`. "Every decorated test case will be synced to a dataset example. When you run the test suite, the dataset will be updated and **a new experiment will be created** with one result for each test case." Requires `langsmith>=0.3.4`. — [How to run evaluations with pytest — LangSmith](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/pytest)
- **Gate shape B — a declarative score threshold.** promptfoo's test case exposes `threshold: number` — "**Test will fail if the combined score of assertions is less than this number**." Assertions carry `weight` (default 1); model-graded types (`llm-rubric`, `model-graded-*`) require an LLM provider and support `rubricPrompt` overrides; `assertScoringFunction` combines named assertion scores into the final grade; `derivedMetrics` are "metrics calculated after the eval from named assertion scores." — [Configuration reference — Promptfoo](https://www.promptfoo.dev/docs/configuration/reference/)
- **Gate shape B, wired to a PR.** `promptfoo/promptfoo-action@v1` on `pull_request` with `paths: ['prompts/**']`, `permissions: pull-requests: write` ("used to post comments on Pull Requests"), and an `actions/cache@v4` step on `~/.cache/promptfoo`: "**The cache stores LLM requests and outputs, which can be reused in future runs to save cost.**" Results are "automatically posted to the pull request." Runner needs Node **>= 22.22.0**. — [Testing Prompts with GitHub Actions — Promptfoo](https://www.promptfoo.dev/docs/integrations/github-action/)
- **Gate shape C — a hosted eval run's pass/fail counts.** An OpenAI eval is `data_source_config` (a JSON Schema for each item, with `include_sample_schema: true` to expose `{{ sample.output_text }}`) plus `testing_criteria` (graders). A run returns `result_counts` (`total` / `errored` / `failed` / `passed`), `per_testing_criteria_results`, `per_model_usage`, and a `report_url`. Ground truth lives in the item: `{{ item.correct_label }}` is "a 'ground truth' output that the model should match, **provided by a human**." — [Working with evals — OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evals)
- **⚠️ But do not build on it.** "OpenAI is deprecating the Evals platform. Existing evals content remains available during the transition window. **Evals will become read-only for existing users on October 31, 2026, and the platform is scheduled to shut down on November 30, 2026.**" The deprecations page confirms: announced June 3, 2026; read-only Oct 31, 2026; "The Evals dashboard and API are scheduled to shut down" Nov 30, 2026; "**Graders documented for eval workflows are part of this transition.**" — [Evaluation best practices — OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices) · [Deprecations — OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)
- **OpenAI's own migration path is Promptfoo** — the deprecations entry links "Moving from OpenAI Evals to Promptfoo." — [Deprecations — OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations) · [Moving from OpenAI Evals to Promptfoo — OpenAI Cookbook](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/evaluation/moving-from-openai-evals-to-promptfoo)
- **Grader mechanics still worth teaching** (the concepts outlive the endpoint): graders "compare reference answers to the corresponding model-generated answer and **return a grade in the range from 0 to 1. It's sometimes helpful to give the model partial credit for an answer, rather than a binary 0 or 1**." Types: string check, text similarity, **score model** (LLM judge), Python code execution; templating via `{{ item.* }}` (your dataset) and `{{ sample.* }}` (the model's output). — [Graders — OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/graders)
- **Where to put the gate in the system.** OpenAI's architecture ladder — single-turn → workflow → single-agent → multi-agent — is a map of *where nondeterminism enters*: "Read about each architecture below to identify where nondeterminism enters your system. **That's where you'll want to implement evals.**" Each nondeterministic seam gets its own eval (instruction following, functional correctness), rather than one end-to-end score. — [Evaluation best practices — OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices)

## Concept IDs

Scoped tightly to this day. Deliberately excluded: tracing/spans and golden signals (w07d1), guardrails/output validation implementation (w07d3-adjacent), SLOs and error budgets (w07d4), red-teaming and safety governance (W8).

- **`offline-vs-online-eval`** — The target-based split: curated dataset examples with reference outputs (offline, pre-deploy, regression/benchmark/backtest) vs live production runs and threads with no reference (online, post-deploy, monitoring/anomaly/feedback); reference-free vs reference-based evaluators; the promote-production-traces-into-the-dataset loop; sampling rates, run filters, request-rate ceilings, and the retention/billing side effects of grading live traffic.
- **`llm-as-judge-bias`** — Position bias (order flips verdicts; balanced position calibration), verbosity/length bias (regress it out; length-controlled comparison), self-preference/self-enhancement bias (self-recognition causally drives it; never let the generator grade itself), plus the wider 12-bias picture and the standard mitigations (swap-and-average, force-then-discard reasoning, constrained output space, pairwise over absolute, few-shot grader prompts).
- **`judge-human-agreement`** — Treating the judge as an instrument that must be calibrated: measuring judge↔human agreement (>80% for GPT-4 on MT-Bench, the same level humans reach with each other; ICC 0.47 vs clinician-clinician 0.43 in MedHELM), measuring inter-annotator agreement among the experts themselves, and tracking agreement over time as its own metric so a drifting judge is detectable.
- **`expert-labeled-gold-set`** — Curating `(input, expert label)` pairs with the client because only the clinician/compliance officer can produce a valid label; criteria drift (grading outputs is how the criteria get discovered, so no a-priori rubric); annotation-queue mechanics (prescribed rubrics, multiple reviewers, reservations, pairwise queues, assertions, export-to-dataset); sizing (5-10 → 10-20 → volume) and composition (typical + edge + adversarial, matched to production traffic); dataset versioning and splits.
- **`eval-regression-gate`** — Converting an eval score into a build-breaking control: numeric, per-metric thresholds derived from success criteria; the three implementation shapes (test-runner assertions, declarative `threshold` config in CI with PR comments and response caching, hosted run `result_counts`); pinning the dataset version; deterministic-first grader selection to keep the gate fast and cheap; and gating at each nondeterministic seam rather than end-to-end.

## Client hook

### Healthcare — Riverbend Community Health (Dr. Maya Okonkwo), Week 7

Her ask this week is two-part: *"Give me visibility — and make sure that AI summary thing can't say anything crazy."* The handed-over materials include the LLM summary endpoint with **no output validation** and a transcript where **it invented a medication the patient isn't on**. The W7 deliverable is one instrumented path plus **one content guardrail** on the LLM summary (grounding/validation + refuse-unknown).

**What this day answers:** *how do you know the guardrail works, and how do you prove it still works next month?* A guardrail with no eval is an assertion, not a control — which is exactly the pattern the students already flagged in W1 (the README that self-asserts "HIPAA compliant"). Specifically:

- **The hallucinated-medication transcript is gold-set example #1.** It is a real production trace with a known-bad label. The day's move is to turn that one incident into a permanent, versioned offline example rather than a one-off patch.
- **Only Dr. Okonkwo's clinicians can label "is this summary safe."** "Invented a medication," "dropped an allergy," "contradicted the chart" are clinical-safety judgments an FDE cannot make. This is the concrete case for `expert-labeled-gold-set` — run an annotation session over 20-30 real summaries with a clinician, derive the rubric from what they flag (criteria drift), and expect the clinicians to disagree with each other (MedHELM's ICC 0.43 gives students permission to expect that and a way to measure it).
- **The guardrail must be gated, not just shipped.** `eval-regression-gate`: groundedness/hallucination score on the clinician-labeled set has a floor; a prompt or model change that drops below it fails the build. This is what makes the guardrail auditable — a named metric, a named threshold, and a clinician who owns the labels.
- **Online eval is the other half of "I'm always the last to know."** The incident note is *"intake froze ~20 min, no alert fired."* The AI-summary equivalent is *"it invented a medication and nobody knew until a clinician noticed."* A reference-free online evaluator (groundedness against the source encounter, refuse-unknown compliance) on sampled live summary traces is the same missing-detection story as the missing latency alert — the topic completes her visibility ask instead of sitting next to it.
- **PHI caveat the students should surface themselves:** online eval writes judge output about encounter content into the telemetry store, and evaluated traces get retention-upgraded. That is a new PHI surface, which foreshadows W8's BAA/de-identification finding.

### Finance — Meridian Lending Co. (Dana, VP of Lending Ops), Week 7

Her ask is *"Payments feel flaky and finance complains about 'noise' at month-end. Give me visibility so I'm not the last to know."* The materials are a month of `payments` rows and a processor **settlement file that don't tie out**, plus double-fund events buried in untraced logs. The deliverable is tracing on the payment span, a **daily reconciliation job + break-report**, and one alert. Sam (Controller) is in the room.

**What this day answers:** the reconciliation control and the eval harness are the *same construct*, and naming that is the teaching moment.

- **The settlement file is a reference output — this is an offline eval with deterministic ground truth.** `offline-vs-online-eval` and the "cheapest grader that works" rule land hard here: reconciliation needs **no judge at all**. A month of labeled breaks is a gold set; the daily recon job scored against it is a regression gate. Students should come away knowing that reaching for an LLM judge when a deterministic comparison exists is the mistake, not the sophistication.
- **The break threshold is a regression gate wearing a SOX costume.** `eval-regression-gate` gives Dana and Sam the exact vocabulary an auditor wants: a named metric (variance between ledger and settlement), a numeric threshold, an owner, and an automated failure. "Month-end noise" becomes "the control fired."
- **The judge-needed label in this account is reason accuracy — and only Priya can produce it.** The W3 finding (adverse-action reason rendered as the generic "purchasing history," no record of the real model drivers) and the W8 governance package both hinge on the question *"is this reason the actual reason?"* That is not an engineering judgment; it is a Reg B compliance judgment. `expert-labeled-gold-set` says the reason-accuracy gold set gets built **with Priya**, sample by sample, and `judge-human-agreement` says you then measure how well an automated reason-accuracy check agrees with her before you trust it at scale. This is the day that makes W8's "fair-lending monitoring spec" buildable rather than aspirational.
- **Online eval maps directly to the untraced double-funds.** A reference-free production check on the payment path is the same shape as the online groundedness check on the healthcare side: continuous, sampled, filtered to the interesting runs (retries, breaks), and it catches the class of defect that only shows up in live traffic.
