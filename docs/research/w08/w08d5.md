# w08d5 — Incidents, runbooks, error budgets, and reliability readout

**Verified:** 2026-10-14  
**Authority:** provisionally authoritative LTM derivative, Week 8 FRI and delivery #8; first-party SRE/incident sources and reviewed observability archive.

## Source-assigned scope

Observability-stack review; full-stack reliability delivery; on-call runbook; debugging-with-traces retrospective; executive and technical readouts. Include incidents, rollback/degraded mode, cost/latency/reliability tradeoffs and evidence-backed decisions.

## Reviewed claims

- Error budget is `100% - SLO`; policy links budget consumption to release or reliability action. Burn rate measures the speed of budget consumption and supports multiwindow alerting rather than paging on every spike. — [Implementing SLOs](https://sre.google/workbook/implementing-slos/), [Alerting on SLOs](https://sre.google/workbook/alerting-on-slos/)
- Incident response needs declared roles, communication, mitigation, recovery and learning. A runbook begins with symptom and consequence, then safe diagnostics, decision branches, expected evidence, escalation and verification. — [Google SRE incident management](https://sre.google/workbook/incident-response/)
- Blameless postmortems seek contributing conditions and actionable prevention rather than hiding human decisions. — [Google SRE postmortem culture](https://sre.google/sre-book/postmortem-culture/)
- Cost, latency and quality can move in opposite directions. A recommendation names the SLO protected, the signal changed, the client outcome, residual risk and flip condition.
- The Friday artifact is evidence: representative trace, linked metrics/logs, SLO and budget status, evaluation/guardrail signal, incident timeline, runbook decision, rollback or degrade evidence, and owner.
