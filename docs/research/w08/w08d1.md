## Source-assigned scope

Probabilistic versus deterministic behavior; latency, cost, tokens, errors, hallucination and retrieval quality; SLOs for AI features. Preserve the reviewed distinction between an SLI, an SLO and an SLA and the error-budget decision loop.

## Reviewed claims

- Google's four golden signals are latency, traffic, errors and saturation. Its error definition includes explicit failures, wrong-content successes and by-policy failures, which lets quality and guardrail outcomes become reliability signals when definitions are reviewable. — [Monitoring distributed systems](https://sre.google/sre-book/monitoring-distributed-systems/)
- An SLI measures behavior, an SLO sets a target, and an SLA adds consequences. Ratio-style SLIs and an explicit error-budget policy connect user outcome to release decisions. — [Service level objectives](https://sre.google/sre-book/service-level-objectives/), [Implementing SLOs](https://sre.google/workbook/implementing-slos/)
- 100% is usually the wrong reliability target; the objective must balance user need, engineering cost and change velocity. — [Embracing risk](https://sre.google/sre-book/embracing-risk/)
- Hallucination rate and retrieval quality require a labeled or otherwise defensible evaluation contract. They are not inferred from HTTP status or model confidence.
- Cost is derived from dated prices and measured usage, with assumptions visible. This lesson uses synthetic values only and makes no current price claim.
