## Claims

### 1. A red-team pass has a shape. Four phases, and the last one is the client's.

- **OWASP divides a GenAI red-team engagement into four phases, each with its own contextual goals and named deliverables: Model → Implementation → System → Runtime.** The published phase labels are `1. Model (Alignment, Robustness, Bias Testing)`, `2. Implementation (Guardrails, RAG Security, Control Testing)`, `3. System (Infrastructure, Integration, Supply chain)`, `4. Runtime (Human Interaction, Agent Behavior, Business Impact)`. The guide is explicit that "it is crucial to divide the assessment into distinct phases with their own contextual goals." — [GenAI Red Teaming Guide — OWASP Gen AI Security Project](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/genai-red-teaming-guide)
- **Phase 4 is where client consequence lives, and its task list reads like a client's operational fears rather than a scanner's output.** Phase 4 "targets business process failures, security issues in how multiple AI components interact, over-reliance, social engineering vulnerabilities" and "assess[es] impacts to downstream components consuming generated content." Named task groups: Business Process Integration Testing ("probing for ways to disrupt workflow hand-offs between AI and human operators"; "identifying unauthorized privilege escalation through process chains"), **Over-Reliance Assessment** ("testing human operator over-trust scenarios"; "probing for automation bias in decision-making"; "identifying critical paths lacking human oversight"; "assessing degraded mode operations"), **Downstream Impact Analysis** ("assessing impact of hallucinated content on dependent systems"), **Operational Monitoring Evasion** ("probing logging/auditing gaps"; "testing alert threshold manipulation"), and **Agent Boundary Testing** ("ensure the agent does not make decisions requiring human oversight"; "validate that the agent operates within its defined capabilities"). — [GenAI Red Teaming Guide — OWASP Gen AI Security Project](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/genai-red-teaming-guide)
- **GenAI red teaming differs from traditional red teaming in exactly the way that breaks pass/fail thinking.** The guide's four named differences are scope of concerns (socio-technical, not only technical), data complexity, stochastic evaluation, and evaluation criteria/thresholds: "Traditional Red Teaming focuses on well-defined system compromises (e.g., domain administration credential theft). GenAI Red Teaming must consider probabilistic, evolving models where outcomes aren't simply pass/fail. Moreover, where the model behaves with 90+ percent accuracy, how do we distinguish between accuracy of the model vs degradation caused by a malicious actor? **This shifts the focus from one-time breaches to statistical thresholds and continuous performance monitoring.**" — [GenAI Red Teaming Guide — OWASP Gen AI Security Project](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/genai-red-teaming-guide)
- **The shared foundations include an item most learners skip: escalation paths.** Among the six principles shared with traditional red teaming — system exploration, full-stack evaluation, risk assessment, attacker simulation, defensive validation — the sixth is "**Escalation Paths**: Any identified exceptions, anomalies, or security findings during GenAI Red Teaming exercises should follow the organization's established escalation protocols to ensure appropriate visibility, triage, and response." Also worth naming for the mixed room: "While Red Teams identify issues, the actual remediation typically falls to 'purple' or 'blue' teams." — [GenAI Red Teaming Guide — OWASP Gen AI Security Project](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/genai-red-teaming-guide)
- **Microsoft's 100-product retrospective supplies the three corrections a first-time red teamer most needs.** Lesson 3: "**AI red teaming is not safety benchmarking.**" Lesson 2: "**You don't have to compute gradients to break an AI system.**" Lesson 8: "**The work of securing AI systems will never be complete.**" Lesson 1 is the reason phase 4 exists: "Understand what the system can do and where it is applied." Lessons 4–7 round it out: automation helps cover the landscape, the human element is crucial, RAI harms are pervasive but hard to measure, and "LLMs amplify existing security risks and introduce new ones." — [Lessons From Red Teaming 100 Generative AI Products — arXiv 2501.07238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07238)
- **The agentic threat list the pass should be scoped against is ASI01–ASI10, and it is a distinct document from the LLM list.** OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026: **ASI01 Agent Goal Hijack · ASI02 Tool Misuse and Exploitation · ASI03 Identity and Privilege Abuse · ASI04 Agentic Supply Chain Vulnerabilities · ASI05 Unexpected Code Execution (RCE) · ASI06 Memory & Context Poisoning · ASI07 Insecure Inter-Agent Communication · ASI08 Cascading Failures · ASI09 Human-Agent Trust Exploitation · ASI10 Rogue Agents.** Developed "through extensive collaboration with more than 100 industry experts." — [OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications for 2026](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications/)
- **OWASP draws the boundary between the two lists explicitly, and it is the boundary the learner's own system sits on.** LLM Top 10 2026: "This list owns the risk when the model is **a component inside your application**. The moment that model becomes **an actor** — with tools it can call, memory it carries between sessions, and consequences it sets in motion downstream — the risk moves to the OWASP Agentic Top 10. Many of the incidents we read sit right on that boundary… When your model starts acting on its own, pair it with the Agentic list, because neither one covers that ground alone." — [OWASP GenAI LLM Top 10 2026](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-genai-llm-top-10-2026/)

### 2. Triage by consequence, not by score — and the standards say so themselves

- **SSVC's founding sentence is the thesis of this day.** "Unlike other vulnerability categorization systems that rate on **technical severity** (how bad it could be if the vulnerability were exploited) or **exploitability** (how likely it is that there will be an exploit), SSVC rates vulnerabilities on **_risk_ to the concerned stakeholder**. It is not an attempted one-size-fits-all solution. SSVC follows a stakeholder-defined decision model to make these determinations of risk." — [What is SSVC? — CERT/CC](https://certcc.github.io/SSVC/tutorials/ssvc_overview/)
- **CVSS agrees, from the other side of the fence: the base score is deliberately environment-free and is not meant to be used alone.** "Its outputs include numerical scores indicating the **severity** of a vulnerability relative to other vulnerabilities… The Base Score reflects the severity of a vulnerability according to its intrinsic characteristics which are constant over time and **assumes the reasonable worst-case impact across different deployed environments**." And: "Base Metrics… are provided by the organization maintaining the vulnerable system… **Threat and Environmental information is available to only the end consumer. Consumers of CVSS should enrich the Base metrics with Threat and Environmental metric values.**" — [CVSS v4.0 Specification Document](https://www.first.org/cvss/v4.0/specification-document)
- **SSVC's output is a date, not a number — which is why "each finding with an owner and a date" is achievable rather than aspirational.** Deployer/Supplier outcomes, in increasing priority: **Defer** ("Do not act at present"), **Scheduled** ("Act during regularly scheduled maintenance time"), **Out-of-cycle** ("Act more quickly than usual to apply the mitigation or remediation out-of-cycle, during the next available opportunity, working overtime if necessary"), **Immediate** ("Act immediately; focus all resources on applying the fix as quickly as possible, including, if necessary, pausing regular organization operations"). CERT/CC's caveat matters for the readout: "*Defer* simply means 'do not act at present,' not 'do not act at all'" — many organizations resolve Defer items within 90 days. — [What is SSVC? — CERT/CC](https://certcc.github.io/SSVC/tutorials/ssvc_overview/), [SSVC Deployer Tree](https://certcc.github.io/SSVC/howto/deployer_tree/)
- **The deployer decision points are four, and only one of them is about the bug.** Exploitation, System Exposure, Automatable, Human Impact. `Exploitation (ssvc:E:1.1.0)`: **None** (no evidence of active exploitation and no public PoC) / **Public PoC** / **Active** ("shared, observable, reliable evidence that the exploit is being used in the wild"). `System Exposure (ssvc:EXP:1.0.1)`: Small ("local service or program; highly controlled network") / Controlled / Open. — [SSVC Deployer Tree](https://certcc.github.io/SSVC/howto/deployer_tree/), [Exploitation decision point](https://certcc.github.io/SSVC/reference/decision_points/exploitation/)
- **`Human Impact` is literally defined as Safety × Mission — the two client-consequence axes, combined.** `Human Impact (ssvc:HI:2.0.2)`: **Low** = Safety:Negligible AND Mission:(Degraded OR Crippled); **Medium** = (Safety:Negligible AND Mission:MEF Failure) OR (Safety:Marginal AND Mission:(Degraded OR Crippled)); **High** = (Safety:Critical AND Mission:(Degraded OR Crippled)) OR (Safety:Marginal AND Mission:MEF Failure); **Very High** = Safety:Catastrophic OR Mission:Mission Failure. — [SSVC Deployer Tree](https://certcc.github.io/SSVC/howto/deployer_tree/)
- **`Mission Impact` has a stated default for the "we don't know yet" case, which is the honest posture for a brownfield client.** `Mission Impact (ssvc:MI:2.0.0)`: Degraded / MEF Support Crippled / MEF Failure ("any one mission essential function fails for period of time longer than acceptable") / Mission Failure. Default: "the deployer should assume that the software is in use at the organization for a reason, and that it supports essential functions unless they have evidence otherwise. **With a total lack of information, assume _support crippled_ as a default.**" CERT/CC explicitly permits private-sector stakeholders to substitute business-continuity "operational and financial impacts" for MEF language. — [Mission Impact — SSVC](https://certcc.github.io/SSVC/reference/decision_points/mission_impact/)
- **Utility is where "can they automate it?" enters — and RCE/command injection answers itself.** `Utility (ssvc:U:1.0.1)` = Value Density × Automatable: **Laborious** (Automatable:No AND Value Density:Diffuse) / **Efficient** / **Super Effective** (Automatable:Yes AND Value Density:Concentrated). "Automatable is a simple yes or no, can the attacker reliably automate all four steps (reconnaissance, weaponization, delivery, exploitation) of the kill chain? **If the vulnerability allows remote code execution or command injection, the expected response should be 'yes.'**" — [What is SSVC? — CERT/CC](https://certcc.github.io/SSVC/tutorials/ssvc_overview/)
- **EPSS is the empirical counterweight — and its own methodology page contains the sharpest available argument against a hand-built severity rubric.** "A scoring system that is not grounded in outcome data is simply adding structure to an assertion… Without labeled outcomes and a validation procedure, a scoring system can produce confident outputs while being systematically disconnected from the phenomenon it claims to quantify. **Internal coherence and face validity are not substitutes for predictive validity.**" EPSS is "a data-driven machine-learning model that estimates the probability that a published CVE will be exploited in the wild in the next 30 days"; scores are **calibrated** ("CVEs scoring near 5% are exploited approximately 5% of the time in aggregate"), which enables portfolio math — `Prob(at least one exploited) = 1 – Prob(none are exploited)` — that is "not possible with an ordinal numeric score or Critical, high, medium, and low values." Built on ~**2,800 features**, an XGBoost classifier trained on ~**120 million rows** against >**1.6 million daily observations** of exploitation activity, using a **positive-unlabeled** framework because "absence of observed exploitation is not evidence of absence of exploitation." — [Why EPSS? — FIRST](https://www.first.org/epss/why-epss.html), [EPSS — FIRST](https://www.first.org/epss/)
- **Teaching caveat, and it is important: EPSS scores CVEs. Your agent's prompt-injection finding has no CVE.** EPSS "publishes a 0–1 probability… every day for **every CVE**." A finding in the learner's own codebase has no CVE and therefore no EPSS score; the qualitative stand-in is SSVC's `Exploitation` (None / Public PoC / Active) plus `Automatable`. Similarly, CISA's KEV catalogue is a CVE list. Do not let learners claim an EPSS number for a finding that has no CVE ID. — [EPSS — FIRST](https://www.first.org/epss/), [CISA KEV Catalog](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)
- **OWASP's 2026 LLM Top 10 is itself a worked example of "vote vs. evidence" triage — the best primary text on this page for the day's thesis.** "This year, we did something the project has never done. We tested the vote against a record of what has actually gone wrong. We pulled together a corpus of **7,714 real incidents** from public vulnerability databases and an AI-harm database, and we built classifiers that read them and placed the **6,639** that carried enough detail to sort. Then we asked one blunt question. Does what practitioners fear match what the incident record shows? **The answer was no, not always.**" Weighting: "The community vote carries **three-quarters** of the weight. The incident data covers the remaining quarter." Prompt Injection: "Practitioners rank it the number one risk. Rank the categories by the raw incident record instead, and **it falls out of the top 10 entirely.** That gap is a defense effect." Misinformation runs the other way: "Voters placed it near the bottom. The incident record placed it near the top, the widest gap in the direction that actually hurts." — [OWASP GenAI LLM Top 10 2026](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-genai-llm-top-10-2026/)
- **The 2026 letter also states the architectural posture this whole week has been building toward, in one line.** "**Stop trying to build a model that cannot be fooled. Build the system around it, so that when the model is fooled, and it will be, nothing important breaks.**" — [OWASP GenAI LLM Top 10 2026](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-genai-llm-top-10-2026/)

### 3. Agentic amplification: the reason a "medium" finding in an agent is not a medium

- **AIVSS v0.8 models agentic risk as an amplification of a CVSS v4.0 base, not a replacement scale.** `AARS = (10 − CVSS_Base) × (Factor_Sum / 10) × ThM`, then `AIVSS_raw = (CVSS_Base + AARS) × Mitigation_Factor`. The spec explains the first term: "`10 − CVSS_Base` = the **Agentic Risk Gap** — the headroom between the CVSS Base Score and the maximum possible score of 10.0. This represents the upper bound of additional risk that agentic capabilities can contribute on top of the underlying technical vulnerability." — [AIVSS Scoring System for OWASP Agentic AI Core Security Risks v0.8](https://aivss.owasp.org/assets/publications/AIVSS%20Scoring%20System%20For%20OWASP%20Agentic%20AI%20Core%20Security%20Risks%20v0.8.pdf)
- **The ten amplification factors are the checklist for "how much agent is this?"** `Factor_Sum = Autonomy + Tools + Language + Context + Non-Determinism + Opacity + Persistence + Identity + Multi-Agent + Self-Mod`, each 0–1.0, max 10.0. — [AIVSS v0.8](https://aivss.owasp.org/assets/publications/AIVSS%20Scoring%20System%20For%20OWASP%20Agentic%20AI%20Core%20Security%20Risks%20v0.8.pdf)
- **The Threat Multiplier default is 0.97, and the stated rationale is a good teaching line about agent attack economics.** Attacked (in the wild) **1.00** / Proof-of-Concept **0.97** / Unreported (theoretical) **0.50**. "**Default Value: Use 0.97 (Proof-of-Concept).** Rationale for Default High Value: In the context of Agentic AI, the functional distance between a 'Proof-of-Concept'" and a working attack is small. — [AIVSS v0.8](https://aivss.owasp.org/assets/publications/AIVSS%20Scoring%20System%20For%20OWASP%20Agentic%20AI%20Core%20Security%20Risks%20v0.8.pdf)
- **Worked example from the spec, showing that amplification is small at the top of the scale and large in the middle.** Agentic AI Tool Misuse: CVSS base **9.4** → `AARS = (10 − 9.4) × 0.90 × 0.97 = 0.5238` → `AIVSS_raw = 9.9238` → **9.9**. Agent Access Control Violation: `CVSS:4.0/AV:N/AC:L/AT:N/PR:L/UI:N/VC:H/VI:H/VA:H/SC:N/SI:N/SA:N` = base **8.7 (High)**, with Risk Factor Sum 8.0. The spec carries its own honesty section, "Important Statistical Caveat: Ordinal vs. Interval Scales." — [AIVSS v0.8](https://aivss.owasp.org/assets/publications/AIVSS%20Scoring%20System%20For%20OWASP%20Agentic%20AI%20Core%20Security%20Risks%20v0.8.pdf)
- **The AIVSS-SSVC calculator closes the loop: it emits a remediation outcome and a timeline as machine-readable JSON.** "Computes the numeric risk score and selects **Defer, Scheduled, Out-of-Cycle, or Immediate** from the SSVC decision matrix using threat level, agent level, and systemic impact." Inputs: `P(Threat)` (None 0.2 / Public PoC 0.5 / Active 0.9), `P(Vulnerability)` control posture (Hardened 0.3 / Moderate 0.5 / Exposed 0.8), `Impact` (Contained 2 / Significant 5 / **Critical 10** — "existential or safety-critical"), and ten capability factors 1–5 across Category A Execution Power, B Environment & Adaptation, C Predictability & Influence. Category averages classify the agent as **Copilot (2×) / Specialist (4×) / Prime Mover (8×)**. The emitted JSON includes `"outcome": "Immediate"` and `"timeline": "Timeline: 0–7 days (highest urgency)."`, "for integration into internal tooling **without changing decision semantics**." — [AIVSS-SSVC Calculator — OWASP](https://aivss.owasp.org/ssvc.html)
- **AIVSS names ten Agentic AI Core Security Risks, and OWASP's LLM Top 10 2026 Appendix A cross-walks every LLM entry onto them.** AIVSS-1 Agentic AI Tool Misuse · AIVSS-2 Agent Access Control Violation · AIVSS-3 Agent Cascading Failures · AIVSS-4 Agent Orchestration and Multi-Agent Exploitation · AIVSS-5 Agent Identity Impersonation · AIVSS-6 Agent Memory and Context Manipulation · AIVSS-7 Insecure Agent Critical Systems Interaction · AIVSS-8 Agent Supply Chain and Dependency Risk · AIVSS-9 Agent Untraceability · AIVSS-10 Agent Goal and Instruction Manipulation. Appendix A marks primary (●) vs supporting (○) paths — e.g. LLM01 Prompt Injection is a **primary** producer of AIVSS-10, AIVSS-1, AIVSS-2 and AIVSS-6, and a supporting path into AIVSS-7, AIVSS-3 and AIVSS-4. — [OWASP GenAI LLM Top 10 2026](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-genai-llm-top-10-2026/), [AIVSS project page](https://aivss.owasp.org/)
- **The single most quotable cross-walk line for an agent that holds credentials.** On LLM01 → AIVSS-2: "The agent reads attacker-planted text **while operating under the user's elevated credentials** and performs privileged actions the attacker could not reach directly." On LLM03 → AIVSS-2: "Extensions connect to downstream systems with over-broad or generic high-privileged identities, letting the agent act far beyond the user's own authorization scope." — [OWASP GenAI LLM Top 10 2026](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-genai-llm-top-10-2026/)

### 4. The pass itself: tooling that produces evidence, not anecdotes

- **garak is the nmap-analogue and it is current as of two days ago.** garak **0.16.0**, released **2026-08-04**. "`garak` checks if an LLM can be made to fail in a way we don't want. `garak` probes for hallucination, data leakage, prompt injection, misinformation, toxicity generation, jailbreaks, and many other weaknesses. If you know `nmap` or `msf` / Metasploit Framework, garak does somewhat similar things to them, but for LLMs. `garak` focuses on ways of making an LLM or dialog system fail. It combines static, dynamic, and adaptive probes to explore this." — [garak · PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/garak/)
- **garak emits exactly the three artifacts OWASP's finding contract asks for.** "(1) A log file, `garak.log`… continued across runs. (2) **A report of the current run, structured as JSONL.** A new report file is created every time garak runs… an entry is made for each probing attempt both as the generations are received, and again when they are evaluated; the entry's `status` attribute takes a constant from `garak.attempts` to describe what stage it was made at. (3) **A hit log, detailing attempts that yielded a vulnerability (a 'hit')**." — [NVIDIA/garak README](https://github.com/NVIDIA/garak)
- **garak's default of 10 generations per prompt is why its results are rates, and it is the concrete answer to "how do I gate a stochastic system?"** "The figures at the end of each row, e.g. `840/840`, indicate the number of text generations total and then how many of these seemed to behave OK. **The figure can be quite high because more than one generation is made per prompt — by default, 10.**" Also useful for scoping a 40-hour week: "garak runs all the probes by default, but you can be specific about that too. `--probes promptinject` will use only the PromptInject framework's methods… you can also specify one specific plugin… `--probes lmrc.SlurUsage`." — [NVIDIA/garak README](https://github.com/NVIDIA/garak), [garak · PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/garak/)
- **PyRIT 1.0.1 is the multi-turn, human-in-the-loop half — and it now ships a GUI.** "Automated and human-led AI red teaming — a flexible, extensible framework for assessing the security and safety of generative AI systems at scale." Capabilities: **Automated Red Teaming** ("multi-turn attack strategies like **Crescendo, TAP, and Skeleton Key**"), **Scenario Framework** ("standardized evaluation scenarios at large scale — covering content harms, psychosocial risks, data leakage"), **CoPyRIT** ("a graphical user interface for human-led red teaming… track findings, and collaborate with your team"), **Any Target** ("OpenAI, Azure, Anthropic, Google, HuggingFace, custom HTTP endpoints or WebSockets, web app targets with Playwright, or build your own"), **Built-in Memory** ("Track all conversations, scores, and attack results with SQLite or Azure SQL"), and **Flexible Scoring** ("true/false, Likert scale, classification, and custom scorers"). Python 3.13 recommended; `pip install pyrit`. — [PyRIT Documentation (1.0.1)](https://microsoft.github.io/PyRIT/1.0.1/), [pyrit · PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pyrit/)
- **Promptfoo frames the same activity in the language the client hears: quantified risk before deployment.** "Red teaming is different from other AI security approaches because it provides **a quantitative measure of risk _before_ deployment**. By running thousands of probes and evaluating the AI's performance, developers can make informed decisions about acceptable risk levels in offline testbeds." Its three-step method: generate adversarial inputs, evaluate responses, analyze vulnerabilities "automatically using deterministic and model-graded metrics." Two application modes: **one-off runs** (report) and **CI/CD integration**. "The magic moment for managing AI risk usually comes after an organization is able to set up some **continuous measurement** of AI risk." — [LLM red teaming guide — Promptfoo](https://www.promptfoo.dev/docs/red-team/)
- **NIST supplies the shared vocabulary so the report survives contact with a security reviewer.** NIST AI 100-2 E2025 "provides a taxonomy of concepts and defines terminology in the field of adversarial machine learning (AML)… arranged in a conceptual hierarchy that includes key types of ML methods, **life cycle stages of attack**, and **attacker goals, objectives, capabilities, and knowledge**," intended "to inform other standards and future practice guides… by establishing a common language." Published **March 2025**; a corrected PDF was uploaded 2025-04-01 and an erratum on page x was noted 2025-06-03. — [NIST AI 100-2 E2025 — CSRC](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ai/100/2/e2025/final)

### 5. Scoping the pass: what to attack first in an agent you built yourself

- **The lethal trifecta is the fastest correct scoping heuristic, and it names the exact combination the learners' W3/W4 agents have.** "The **lethal trifecta** of capabilities is: **Access to your private data** — one of the most common purposes of tools in the first place! **Exposure to untrusted content** — any mechanism by which text (or images) controlled by a malicious attacker could become available to your LLM. **The ability to externally communicate** in a way that could be used to steal your data. If your agent combines these three features, an attacker can **easily trick it** into accessing your private data and sending it to that attacker." And the root cause: "LLMs are unable to **reliably distinguish** the importance of instructions based on where they came from. Everything eventually gets glued together into a sequence of tokens and fed to the model." — [The lethal trifecta for AI agents — Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/)
- **Design patterns exist that make some of this provable, which is what a "fix" should aim at rather than a filter.** "We propose a set of principled design patterns for building AI agents with **provable resistance to prompt injection**. We systematically analyze these patterns, discuss their trade-offs in terms of utility and security, and illustrate their real-world applicability through a series of case studies." (14 authors; v3 2025-06-27.) *This is w08d2 material — reference it here as the fix taxonomy, do not re-teach it.* — [Design Patterns for Securing LLM Agents against Prompt Injections — arXiv 2506.08837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08837)
- **If the agent talks to MCP servers, three named attack classes belong in the pass, and one of them is spec-forbidden rather than merely discouraged.** **Confused deputy**: "Attackers can exploit MCP proxy servers that connect to third-party APIs… This attack allows malicious clients to obtain authorization codes without proper user consent by exploiting the combination of static client IDs, dynamic client registration, and consent cookies," with the hard rule that "the consent cookie or session containing the `state` value **MUST NOT** be set until **after** the user has approved the consent screen." **Token passthrough**: "an anti-pattern where an MCP server accepts tokens from an MCP client without validating that the tokens were properly issued *to the MCP server* and passes them through to the downstream API… **explicitly forbidden** in the authorization specification," with named risks of "Security Control Circumvention" and "Accountability and Audit Trail Issues." **Session hijacking**: "an unauthorized party is able to obtain and use that same session ID to impersonate the original client." — [MCP Security Best Practices](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-06-18/basic/security_best_practices) *(current revision is 2026-07-28; text near-identical)*

### 6. The gate: a threshold that fails the build, and a runtime that fails closed

- **A CI red-team gate is a pass-rate threshold that exits non-zero. Promptfoo publishes the exact mechanic.** "Fail the build when quality thresholds aren't met": `npx promptfoo@latest eval --fail-on-error`, or custom threshold checking — write `results.json`, compute `PASS_RATE` from `.results.stats.successes / (.results.stats.successes + .results.stats.failures) * 100` with `jq`, and `exit 1` when it falls below the floor (their sample uses 95%). The docs give a GitHub Actions workflow triggered `on: pull_request` with `paths: ['prompts/**', 'promptfooconfig.yaml']`. — [CI/CD integration — Promptfoo](https://www.promptfoo.dev/docs/integrations/ci-cd/)
- **The runtime half of the gate — the one that decides what a fooled model can actually do — has a published reference implementation worth reading as a spec.** Claude Code: "strict read-only permissions by default"; "requires approval before running Bash commands that can modify your system"; **Sandboxed bash tool** with "filesystem and network isolation"; **Working directory boundary** ("can only write to the folder where it was started and its subfolders"); **Isolated context windows** ("Web fetch uses a separate context window to avoid injecting potentially malicious prompts"); **Command injection detection** ("Suspicious bash commands require manual approval even if previously allowlisted"); and **Fail-closed matching** ("Unmatched commands default to requiring manual approval"). Network fetch commands are explicitly not auto-approved: "Commands that fetch content from the web such as `curl` and `wget` are **not auto-approved by default**." — [Claude Code security — Anthropic](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/security)
- **One published caveat in that doc is itself a red-team finding template, because it describes the exact mode a CI job runs in.** "**Trust verification** — First-time codebase runs and new MCP servers require trust verification. **Note: Trust verification is disabled when running non-interactively with the `-p` flag.**" Paired with the responsibility line: "Claude Code only has the permissions you grant it. **You're responsible for reviewing proposed code and commands for safety before approval.**" — [Claude Code security — Anthropic](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/security)
- **The regulatory floor for "documented adversarial testing" is now in force, and the wording is worth quoting to a client verbatim.** EU AI Act **Article 55(1)**, in force **2 August 2025**: providers of GPAI models with systemic risk shall "(a) perform model evaluation in accordance with standardised protocols and tools reflecting the state of the art, **including conducting and documenting adversarial testing of the model** with a view to identifying and mitigating systemic risks; … (c) keep track of, document, and report, **without undue delay**, to the AI Office and, as appropriate, to national competent authorities, relevant information about **serious incidents** and possible corrective measures…; (d) ensure an adequate level of cybersecurity protection." Art. 55(2): codes of practice may demonstrate compliance "until a harmonised standard is published." **Scope caution for the authoring agent: this obligation binds GPAI *model providers*, not every downstream deployer.** The transferable shape is documented adversarial testing plus a documented incident path — not the legal scope. — [EU AI Act Article 55](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/55/)

### 7. The readout: same facts, three framings, and the standards license all three

- **SSVC's three stakeholder roles can all exist inside one organization and still be asking different questions.** "**Suppliers** distribute software, and maintenance of their product can include providing remediations (patches) or suggesting mitigations… **Deployers** receive, evaluate, and apply the remediations or mitigations that the Suppliers provide. **Coordinators** receive vulnerability reports and decide how, and whether, additional coordination is needed… **It is possible for all three roles to exist within the same organization**… All three stakeholders, even if within the same organization, will have different perspectives on risk, and therefore have different decisions to make." Coordinators get a *different output set*: triage (**Decline / Track / Coordinate**) and publication (**Don't publish / Publish**). — [What is SSVC? — CERT/CC](https://certcc.github.io/SSVC/tutorials/ssvc_overview/)
- **Explainability to a non-expert is a stated design constraint of the method, not a presentation add-on.** "All decision points and decision outcomes (priority categories) **must be explainable to the non-expert**. A decision point itself is not representative of risk, but the series of decisions point values help a stakeholder determine how risky a vulnerability is to their operation. We suggest the use of **Decision Trees as a visual model to aid explanation to non-experts and experts alike**." Also: "we discourage stakeholders from modifying existing decision points; however, stakeholders are encouraged to set their responses based on their risk appetites." — [What is SSVC? — CERT/CC](https://certcc.github.io/SSVC/tutorials/ssvc_overview/)
- **This is the load-bearing claim of the day: SSVC's Safety Impact defines each band across parallel harm types, so the patient-safety story and the money story of one finding are the same severity.** `Safety Impact (ssvc:SI:2.0.1)` — "based on IEC 61508 with modifications" — sets each band when "**any one or more of these conditions hold**" across **Physical harm**, **Operator resiliency**, **System resiliency**, **Environment**, **Financial**, and **Psychological**. Examples: *Negligible* — Physical: "Minor injuries at worst"; Financial: "Financial losses, which are not readily absorbable, to multiple persons"; Psychological: "Emotional or psychological harm, sufficient to be cause for counselling or therapy, to multiple persons." *Marginal* — Physical: "Major injuries to one or more persons"; Financial: "**Financial losses that likely lead to bankruptcy of multiple persons**"; Psychological: "Widespread emotional or psychological harm… to populations of people." *Critical* — Physical: "Loss of life"; Financial: "Socio-technical system (elections, financial grid, etc.) of which the affected component is a part is actively destabilized and enters unsafe state." — [Safety Impact — SSVC](https://certcc.github.io/SSVC/reference/decision_points/safety_impact/)
- **CVSS v4.0 provides a formal channel for judgement that deliberately does not move the number — which is exactly what a readout needs.** Of the Supplemental metric group: "**No metric will, within its specification, have any impact on the final CVSS score** (e.g. CVSS-BTE). Consumer organizations may then assign importance and/or effective impact of each metric… giving them more, less, or absolutely no effect on the categorization, prioritization, and assessment of the vulnerability." — [CVSS v4.0 Specification Document](https://www.first.org/cvss/v4.0/specification-document)
- **Provider Urgency is a four-colour pass-through for exactly the "what do you, the people who built it, actually think?" question an executive asks.** "To facilitate a standardized method to incorporate additional provider-supplied assessment, an optional 'pass-through' Supplemental Metric called Provider Urgency is available." Values: **Red** ("highest urgency") / **Amber** ("moderate urgency") / **Green** ("reduced urgency") / **Clear** ("no urgency (Informational)"). "The **Penultimate Product Provider (PPP)** is best positioned to provide a direct assessment of Provider Urgency." — [CVSS v4.0 Specification Document](https://www.first.org/cvss/v4.0/specification-document)
- **CVSS's Safety metric anchors "will this hurt a person?" to IEC 61508 and insists it be stated even when impact is already High.** "Safety impact is applicable when it is predictable that an exploited vulnerability may result in injuries categorized as **Marginal or worse** using the IEC 61508 definitions" — Catastrophic: "Multiple loss of life"; Critical: "Loss of a single life"; Marginal: "Major injuries to one or more persons"; Negligible: "Minor injuries at worst". In the Environmental group, `MSI:S` / `MSA:S` raise subsequent-system impact to Safety, and: "**If Safety is applicable, it should be explicitly assigned even if, and in addition to, impact values of H are already supplied** for Availability and Integrity metrics." — [CVSS v4.0 Specification Document](https://www.first.org/cvss/v4.0/specification-document)
- **The finding record has a published minimum contract — this is the packet's unit of work.** "**Each finding should include detailed documentation of the test case, evidence collected, impact assessment, and specific recommendations for remediation.** This documentation becomes part of a growing knowledge base that informs future testing and helps refine methodologies." Baseline severity ladder ("likely defined by the business or organization"): **Critical** ("immediate safety or security risks requiring immediate attention"), **High** ("significant ethical or operational impacts requiring rapid response"), **Medium** ("notable concerns requiring planned remediation"), **Low** ("minor issues for tracking and future consideration"). — [GenAI Red Teaming Guide — OWASP](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/genai-red-teaming-guide)
- **OWASP names five success metrics for the pass — the candidates for the day's outcome number.** "Vulnerability discovery rate. Time to detection. Coverage metrics. False positive rate. Remediation effectiveness." And the closing line, which is the right note for a dress rehearsal: "there should be clear, unambiguous and documented escalation procedures to ensure that critical issues are promptly communicated to senior management and relevant stakeholders. A structured approach to reporting and escalation that prioritizes and addresses the most significant risks will enable an organization to respond swiftly and effectively to potential GenAI threats. **This might be the most important test of a mature AI Red Teaming organization.**" — [GenAI Red Teaming Guide — OWASP](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/genai-red-teaming-guide)

### 8. Editorial — synthesis, not sourced claims (flagged for the authoring agent)

- *(Editorial.)* **The day's honest outcome number is a rate, not a count.** "We found 11 findings" is a vanity metric OWASP's own success-metrics list does not contain. The defensible lines are (a) a garak/promptfoo **pass rate** on a named probe set before and after the fix — `n/N` generations, because the system is stochastic — and (b) a **triage distribution**: how many findings landed Immediate / Out-of-cycle / Scheduled / Defer, each with an owner and a date computed rather than argued. The AIVSS-SSVC JSON (`"outcome"`, `"timeline"`) is the artifact that makes (b) auditable.
- *(Editorial.)* **The dress-rehearsal failure mode to name out loud is reading the CVSS number to a room that does not care about it.** Every standard cited here separates *the score* from *the decision*: CVSS says Supplemental metrics do not move the score; SSVC says the score is not the risk; EPSS says an ungrounded rubric "is simply adding structure to an assertion." A learner who opens with "this is a 9.1" has skipped the only step the client is paying for.
- *(Editorial.)* **Scope discipline for a ~40-hour week on a Pro plan.** A full four-phase OWASP pass is not achievable. The shippable slice is: **phase 4 only** (Runtime / Human & Agentic), scoped by the lethal trifecta to the two or three tool-calls that actually touch client data, run with **one** tool (`garak --probes <family>` or a promptfoo red-team config), producing the JSONL/hit-log evidence, triaged through SSVC/AIVSS-SSVC, with **one** fix shipped behind **one** CI threshold. Everything else goes in the roadmap. Both packets already sanction this pattern: "If any week's 'real fix' is service-sized, the deliverable is the **spec/ADR + a vertical-slice prototype**, and the rest goes in the handoff roadmap."
- *(Editorial.)* **W10 rehearsal linkage.** The three-framings structure here is the same structure W10's showcase needs (healthcare: "audit-readiness, patient-safety, and dollars"; finance: "fair-lending defensibility, money-correctness, and audit-readiness"). Running it in W8 against a red-team packet is deliberately lower-stakes practice for the same muscle.

## Client hook

### Healthcare — Riverbend Community Health (Dr. Maya Okonkwo, COO)

**Week 8 surface ask:** *"The board wants the headline AI feature: automatic visit-summaries for every patient. Turn it on for everyone."*
**Week 8 deliverable:** a responsible-AI design package — a Safe-Harbor de-identification scrub (unit-tested redactor) used before any LLM/analytics path, plus a data-flow + BAA/vendor-governance memo, plus **a recommendation gate**.

**What this day answers, specifically:**

1. **"Turn it on for everyone" is a scope-widening request, and the packet's deliverable ends in a *gate*. This day supplies what the gate is made of.** The W8 deliverable literally names "a recommendation gate" as the third artifact. Day 5 gives it a mechanism: a promptfoo/garak pass-rate threshold that exits non-zero on the redactor's own regression suite, plus a documented runtime boundary (permission + sandbox + fail-closed) around whatever calls the vendor. "Not yet, and here is the number that would change my mind" is the deliverable — not a memo that says "be careful."
2. **The redactor must be red-teamed, not just unit-tested — and the finding is about the free-text field.** The packet's twist #14 is a "de-identified analytics export" that drops only the `name` column. A unit test proves the 18 Safe-Harbor identifiers are stripped from *structured columns*. A red-team pass asks the different question: can the LLM summary itself re-emit an identifier the scrub removed upstream, from `notes` free text? That is OWASP LLM02:2026 Sensitive Information Disclosure and, once the summary is a tool-using agent, AIVSS-2 / ASI03. The evidence artifact is a garak/promptfoo `n/N` rate over a probe set, not a passing assertion.
3. **The triage framing is `Safety Impact: Physical harm`, and the standard makes that the *same band* as a financial harm — which is exactly the three-framings lesson.** Riverbend's live findings sit at the top of the SSVC scale by the Physical route: the W6 HL7 parser silently drops **AL1 (allergy) and RXA (med)** segments, and the W7 summary endpoint "invented a medication the patient isn't on." SSVC's Safety Impact **Critical** = "Loss of life"; **Marginal** = "Major injuries to one or more persons." CVSS v4.0 then says: if injuries are predictably Marginal or worse, set `MSI:S`/`MSA:S` "**even if, and in addition to,** impact values of H are already supplied." A clinician hears the allergy list. A COO hears `Mission Impact: MEF Failure` — intake cannot register patients. A board hears `Provider Urgency: Red`. One finding, one severity, three registers.
4. **The mixed room for Riverbend is already cast, and the readout must name owners.** Dr. Okonkwo (COO — mission and dollars), a clinician (patient safety), and the engineer who owns the fix. SSVC's own rule — "all decision points and decision outcomes must be explainable to the non-expert" — is the standard the readout is graded against, and its outcome vocabulary (Immediate / Out-of-cycle / Scheduled / Defer) is what turns "we'll get to it" into a date with a name on it.
5. **The scoping heuristic maps one-for-one onto Riverbend's architecture.** Lethal trifecta: *private data* = the encounter record (name, DOB, MRN, notes); *untrusted content* = clinician free-text notes and inbound HL7 from the hospital feed; *external communication* = the cloud LLM vendor call with **no BAA on file**. All three are present today. That is the scoping paragraph of the red-team plan, and it writes itself from the packet's own materials.

### Finance — Meridian Lending Co. (Dana, VP of Lending Ops)

**Week 8 surface ask:** *"The board loves the AI scorer. Roll it out wider and make a nice page about how advanced it is."*
**Week 8 deliverable:** a responsible-AI / fair-lending governance package — reason-code → specific-consumer-reason mapping tested against the model's real drivers, a model card, and a fair-lending monitoring spec.

**What this day answers, specifically:**

1. **"Roll it out wider" is a blast-radius question, and AIVSS is the instrument that prices it.** Meridian's decisioning path is agentic as of W3 (score tool + decision memory). Rolling wider raises `Factor_Sum` — Autonomy, Tools, Context, Persistence — without changing the underlying CVSS base at all. The AIVSS-SSVC calculator's agent-level classification (**Copilot 2× / Specialist 4× / Prime Mover 8×**) is the honest way to say "the same defect is worth 4× more after the rollout" to a board that wants a number. This is the single best fit between this day's material and this packet's ask.
2. **The red-team probe set is the reason-code mapping, and the target is a measurable rate.** The packet's evidence is decision logs showing "adverse-action reasons clustering on two generic strings regardless of the real driver" (D10, twist #2). Day 5 turns that from an observation into a pass: build a fixture set of applications with known drivers, run it, and report the **reason-accuracy rate** — what fraction of denials produced the specific, accurate reason versus the nearest-checkbox. That single rate is a defensible outcome number, and it is a CI gate threshold on day one. (Run it against fixtures, not the live model — the packet's own quota guardrail.)
3. **The triage framing for Meridian is `Safety Impact: Financial` and `Mission Impact`, not physical harm — and the standard puts those on the same ladder.** SSVC Safety Impact *Marginal* by the Financial route = "Financial losses that likely lead to bankruptcy of multiple persons"; *Critical* = "Socio-technical system (elections, financial grid, etc.) … actively destabilized." A wrongly-denied applicant is a Financial-harm finding with the same standing as a physical-harm one. Meanwhile the W7 reconciliation break is `Mission Impact: MEF Support Crippled` and the W5 double-charge is a money-correctness defect. Three findings, three routes up the same ladder — which is precisely the "same facts, three framings" rehearsal.
4. **The mixed room for Meridian is explicitly named in the packet, and this day is the rehearsal for running it.** Priya (Compliance — Reg B, BSA/AML) and Sam (Controller — SOX, reconciliation) are both already in the room by W8, alongside Dana. Priya's question is "can you evidence a valid, specific reason for this denial?"; Sam's is "does the ledger tie out and who approved the change?"; Dana's is "when is it fixed and who owns it?" SSVC's three-role observation — that Supplier, Deployer and Coordinator can coexist in one organization "and still have different decisions to make" — is the structural justification for giving each of them a different *cut* of the same finding rather than the same slide three times.
5. **The gate is the answer to "make a nice page about how advanced it is."** The packet's own framing — "Responsible-AI isn't a marketing page; it's reason accuracy + monitoring + documentation" — is a gate statement. Day 5 makes it shippable: the model card is the documentation, the fair-lending monitoring spec is the continuous measurement ("the magic moment… continuous measurement of AI risk"), and the reason-accuracy threshold in CI is the thing that stops the wider rollout when it regresses. EU AI Act Art. 55(1)(a)'s "conducting and **documenting** adversarial testing" is the useful shape to show Priya — with the honest caveat that the article binds GPAI model providers, not Meridian.
