### What moved from 2025 → 2026 (the delta a client will ask about)

- The 2025 order was: 01 Prompt Injection, 02 Sensitive Information Disclosure, **03 Supply Chain**, **04 Data and Model Poisoning**, **05 Improper Output Handling**, **06 Excessive Agency**, **07 System Prompt Leakage**, **08 Vector and Embedding Weaknesses**, **09 Misinformation**, **10 Unbounded Consumption**. — [2025 release source listing](https://github.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/tree/main/2025)
- **Excessive Agency climbed 06 → 03**, described as "the most consequential move on the list, because the vote and the record agree that agentic deployments are where the damage is landing." — [LLM00:2026 Preface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM00_Preface.md)
- **Unbounded Consumption rose four places** (10 → 06); **Improper Output Handling fell the furthest**, 05 → 10. — [LLM00:2026 Preface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM00_Preface.md)
- **"What used to be System Prompt Leakage is now Hidden Context Exposure, a broader framework for the same failure to trust information that should have stayed out of reach."** — [LLM00:2026 Preface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM00_Preface.md)
- Entries **absorbed** newer risks rather than spawning thin new categories: cross-modal (image/audio) attacks folded into LLM01; promoted-artifact trust failure into LLM04; fine-tuning subversion into LLM05; "the insecure code that assistants generate at scale" into LLM10. Rationale: "Standing up thin new categories would have splintered the list for no gain." — [LLM00:2026 Preface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM00_Preface.md)

### The methodology change — why the 2026 list can answer "says who?"

- 2026 is the first edition checked against incident data: the project "pulled together a corpus of **7,714 real incidents** from public vulnerability databases and an AI-harm database, and we built classifiers that read them and placed the **6,639** that carried enough detail to sort." — [LLM00:2026 Preface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM00_Preface.md)
- The weighting is explicit and deliberate: **"The community vote carries three-quarters of the weight. The incident data covers the remaining quarter."** A quarter weight "is enough to move an entry a tier when the gap between belief and evidence runs wide. It is not enough to let imperfect data rewrite the list on its own." — [LLM00:2026 Preface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM00_Preface.md)
- **The defense effect, stated outright:** rank categories by the raw incident record and prompt injection "falls out of the top 10 entirely… Teams fight injection hard, so fewer clean exploits reach a public database, and the public count understates the risk that mature teams already spend real money holding off." It stayed at #1 anyway. — [LLM00:2026 Preface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM00_Preface.md)
- The reverse gap: **Misinformation** — "Voters placed it near the bottom. The incident record placed it near the top, the widest gap in the direction that actually hurts." The list seats it in the middle (07) as a compromise between the two signals. — [LLM00:2026 Preface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM00_Preface.md)
- Sensitive Information Disclosure held #2, "the one place at the top where belief and evidence simply agree, and where our confidence is highest." — [LLM00:2026 Preface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM00_Preface.md)

### The governing posture (the single sentence to put on a slide)

- **"Stop trying to build a model that cannot be fooled. Build the system around it, so that when the model is fooled, and it will be, nothing important breaks."** The preface says "that posture runs through all ten entries here." — [LLM00:2026 Preface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM00_Preface.md)
- LLM01 restates it as a design rule: "no reliable prevention mechanism exists today… **Defense is therefore architectural rather than interceptive.** Design the surrounding system on the explicit assumption that the model's instruction boundary will eventually be bypassed." — [LLM01:2026 Prompt Injection](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM01_PromptInjection.md)
- LLM01 distinguishes two classes of control: those that "reduce injection success and are expected to degrade against adaptive attackers," and those that "bound the blast radius once injection succeeds, and **these are what survive** against attackers who can probe the system." — [LLM01:2026 Prompt Injection](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM01_PromptInjection.md)

### The scope boundary — model-as-component vs model-as-actor

- **"This list owns the risk when the model is a component inside your application. The moment that model becomes an actor, with tools it can call, memory it carries between sessions, and consequences it sets in motion downstream, the risk moves to the OWASP Agentic Top 10."** — [LLM00:2026 Preface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM00_Preface.md)
- The boundary is porous by design: "Many of the incidents we read sit right on that boundary… When your model starts acting on its own, pair it with the Agentic list, because neither one covers that ground alone." — [LLM00:2026 Preface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM00_Preface.md)
- Individual entries enforce the boundary in their own scope notes — LLM04 defers agentic supply-chain risk (MCP servers, tool registries) to **ASI04**; LLM09 defers non-geometric agent-memory attacks to **ASI06 Memory and Context Poisoning**; LLM03 notes Excessive Agency "can manifest as ASI02: Tool Misuse & Exploitation, ASI03: Identity & Privilege Abuse and ASI08: Cascading Failures." — [LLM04:2026 Supply Chain](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM04_SupplyChain.md) · [LLM09:2026 Vector and Embedding Weaknesses](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM09_VectorAndEmbeddingWeaknesses.md) · [LLM03:2026 Excessive Agency](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM03_ExcessiveAgency.md)

### Item by item — what each entry means in a deployed system

**LLM01:2026 Prompt Injection**
- The root cause is architectural, not a bug: "LLMs make no architectural distinction between 'instructions' and 'data' (both are tokens on the same stream), so there is no clean equivalent to parameterized queries." — [LLM01](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM01_PromptInjection.md)
- Three deployment-time properties make it worse: **context-window pooling** (system prompt, user input, retrieved docs, tool outputs, history and memory are one token stream with "no enforced trust boundary"), **memory persistence** (an injection written to memory/RAG/vector store "taints every subsequent session that reads from that store"), and **agentic execution** (blast radius extends to "whatever the agent's tools can reach"). — [LLM01](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM01_PromptInjection.md)
- Threat-model it along three axes before choosing mitigations: **delivery surface** (direct input / retrieved content / tool output / tool connection channel / persistent memory), **propagation behavior** (single-shot / multi-step kill-chain / cross-session / self-replicating across agents), and **encoding** (plain text / base64 / invisible Unicode / multimodal-steganographic / low-resource language). — [LLM01](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM01_PromptInjection.md)
- Indirect injection surfaces are graded by trust: **untrusted** (public web, unknown-sender email, search results), **semi-trusted** (issue titles, package READMEs, third-party API responses — "content the user chose to retrieve but did not author"), **trusted** (the developer's own repos, databases, internal docs — where an attacker may have planted content via an unrelated upstream vector such as a public bug-report form). — [LLM01](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM01_PromptInjection.md)
- The structural pattern to name for a client: "the attacker does not need to compromise the backend directly. They place text where the developer's LLM will read it, and the LLM, operating with the developer's privileges, does the work." — [LLM01](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM01_PromptInjection.md)
- The list cites Simon Willison's **"lethal trifecta"** (2025) as a pre-deployment check: an agent that simultaneously has (1) access to private data, (2) exposure to untrusted content, and (3) the ability to communicate externally "has the conditions for high-impact exploitation, and removing any one leg removes them." — [LLM01](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM01_PromptInjection.md)

**LLM02:2026 Sensitive Information Disclosure**
- The disclosure channel "is not only the final answer: tool-call arguments, reasoning traces, retrieved chunks, multimodal output, logs, telemetry, embeddings, and observable inference properties (timing, token length, log-probabilities, confidence, cache-hit behavior) are all disclosure surfaces. Treat each as an output subject to the same classification and redaction rules." — [LLM02](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM02_SensitiveInformationDisclosure.md)
- Four lifecycle phases: **training-time** (memorization, which "scales log-linearly with capacity, duplication, and context length"), **inference-time** (live context leakage), **pipeline-time** (fine-tuning, distillation, synthetic data, gradients, SDKs, observability move data into derived artifacts), **observation-time** (side channels). — [LLM02](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM02_SensitiveInformationDisclosure.md)
- Two structural failures drive most incidents: **oversharing upstream** — "unscoped drives, legacy permissions, and knowledge bases feed RAG with sensitive data the model then retrieves as designed. **The fix is the data surface, not the model**" — and **persistence** — "once data influences weights, embeddings, or adapters it stays extractable after source deletion, straining GDPR Article 17 and CCPA §1798.105 erasure obligations." — [LLM02](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM02_SensitiveInformationDisclosure.md)
- Named applicable regimes: **EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, high-risk obligations from August 2026), GDPR, HIPAA, CCPA/CPRA, ISO/IEC 42001, and NIST AI 600-1.** — [LLM02](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM02_SensitiveInformationDisclosure.md)
- **Aggregation is a disclosure**: "Aggregation across individually-permitted sources (budget + hiring + diligence → a pending M&A target) is a disclosure when policy prohibits the synthesized conclusion." — [LLM02](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM02_SensitiveInformationDisclosure.md)
- Mitigations are **tiered** — Tier 1 Foundational (every deployment), Tier 2 Hardening (regulated / high-sensitivity), Tier 3 Advanced (regulated, classified, high-target) — which is what lets a client scope effort to their risk class rather than buying everything. — [LLM02](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM02_SensitiveInformationDisclosure.md)
- Tier 1 item 3 is the one most systems fail: "**Authorize before retrieval**: enforce document- and chunk-level authorization inside the index query, not at the application layer after retrieval." Also flagged: "regex and blocklist filters fall to cross-lingual, base64, and hex encodings" — use "pattern matching plus NER plus trained classifiers." — [LLM02](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM02_SensitiveInformationDisclosure.md)
- Observability platforms are named as a disclosure surface: "Observability platforms (Langfuse, LangSmith, Datadog LLM Observability) log full prompts, completions, chunks, and traces by default." (Direct tie-back to w07 observability week.) — [LLM02](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM02_SensitiveInformationDisclosure.md)

**LLM03:2026 Excessive Agency**
- Definition: "the vulnerability that enables damaging actions to be performed in response to unexpected, ambiguous or manipulated outputs from an LLM, **regardless of what is causing the LLM to malfunction**." — [LLM03](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM03_ExcessiveAgency.md)
- Three root causes, and the whole entry is organized around them: **excessive functionality, excessive permissions, excessive autonomy**. — [LLM03](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM03_ExcessiveAgency.md)
- The canonical excessive-permissions example is a database identity that "not only has SELECT permissions, but also UPDATE, INSERT and DELETE"; the canonical identity failure is a tool that "accesses downstream systems with a generic high-privileged identity" instead of the current user's. — [LLM03](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM03_ExcessiveAgency.md)
- **Complete mediation** is the load-bearing control: "Implement authorization in logic rather than relying on an LLM to decide if an action is allowed or not… validated against security policies by the tool, by an independent pre-execution policy decision point between the tool and the downstream system, or by the downstream system itself." — [LLM03](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM03_ExcessiveAgency.md)
- **Graduated enforcement** (audit, warn, block, escalate) is the pattern that makes HITL affordable: "a customer service chatbot can auto-process a refund as store credit (which is recoverable), while an irreversible action such as an external payout routes to human approval." — [LLM03](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM03_ExcessiveAgency.md)
- Delegation preserves context: "In delegated or multi-agent workflows, preserve the original user context and authorization scope across chained tool or agent calls, rather than relying only on the permissions of the calling agent or service identity." — [LLM03](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM03_ExcessiveAgency.md)
- Two controls are explicitly labeled as damage-limiting rather than preventive: **monitor tool use** and **rate limiting / circuit breakers**. — [LLM03](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM03_ExcessiveAgency.md)

**LLM04:2026 Supply Chain**
- Scope beyond classic SCA: "in ML the risks extend to third-party pre-trained models, datasets, and model artifacts… The supply chain now includes **model artifacts, provenance, and conversion/merge workflows as first-class attack surfaces**." — [LLM04](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM04_SupplyChain.md)
- **Slopsquatting** is named as a new variant: coding assistants "hallucinate plausible but nonexistent package names at scale (Spracklen et al., 2025), which attackers register in advance… so that unverified AI-suggested dependencies resolve to malicious code." — [LLM04](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM04_SupplyChain.md)
- Safe serialization formats are not sufficient: "a backdoor can be embedded directly in a model's computational graph and persist in formats widely considered safe, such as ONNX," and a crafted model file can exploit parser memory-corruption bugs (heap overflows in llama.cpp GGUF parsing, **CVE-2024-23496**). — [LLM04](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM04_SupplyChain.md)
- **Model Cards are documentation, not provenance:** "Model Cards document a model but do not prove its origin, and a compromised or lookalike supplier account can publish a malicious model under a trusted name." Pipelines that resolve artifacts by a mutable reference (a `latest` tag) instead of an immutable digest are the named failure. — [LLM04](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM04_SupplyChain.md)
- **Signing proves integrity and origin, not safety** — "a validly signed model from a compromised or trusted-but-malicious supplier can still be backdoored" — so combine signing (OpenSSF Model Signing + Sigstore, CoSAI maturity model) with immutable references, provenance policy, SLSA-style release gates, and behavioral evaluation. — [LLM04](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM04_SupplyChain.md)
- Inventory extends SBOM → **AIBOM / ML-BOM** (OWASP CycloneDX ML-BOM, OWASP AIBOM project), with licenses tracked in the same inventory. — [LLM04](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM04_SupplyChain.md)
- **Vendor T&Cs are a listed risk** (#7): "Unclear terms and conditions (T&Cs) and data privacy policies of model operators can lead to sensitive application data being used for model training and subsequent exposure." Mitigation #1 is "carefully vet data sources and suppliers, including T&Cs and privacy policies… re-assess on changes in their security posture or T&Cs." — [LLM04](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM04_SupplyChain.md)
- Quantization is a transformation risk: "model weights can be crafted so the full-precision model evaluates benignly while the quantized artifact exhibits attacker-chosen behavior (Egashira et al., 2025), so full-precision assurances do not transfer to the deployed quantized artifact." — [LLM04](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM04_SupplyChain.md)

**LLM05:2026 Data and Model Poisoning**
- Scope is broader than "training data": poisoning "can occur anywhere data is ingested, transformed, retrieved, or reused, including during pre-training, fine-tuning, embedding creation, retrieval augmentation (RAG), and model distribution." — [LLM05](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM05_DataModelPoisoning.md)
- Why it is expensive: "Unlike typical software vulnerabilities that can be patched by fixing code, poisoning can require data revalidation, retraining, model replacement, or pipeline redesign, which is expensive and operationally disruptive." — [LLM05](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM05_DataModelPoisoning.md)
- The number to put on a slide: "**As few as 250 poisoned documents compromise models from 600M to 13B parameters regardless of dataset size** (Souly et al., 2025)." Poisoning volume does not scale with corpus size. — [LLM05](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM05_DataModelPoisoning.md)
- Poisoning defeats standard evaluation by design: "A targeted variant deliberately erodes refusal behaviors while preserving general accuracy, making degradation undetectable through standard evaluation." Backdoors "may leave model behavior untouched until a trigger causes it to change, creating the opportunity for a model to become a **sleeper agent**." — [LLM05](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM05_DataModelPoisoning.md)
- RAG corpus poisoning: "A single optimized poisoned text injected per targeted query can override accurate content in a retrieval corpus, and the attack retains high success against paraphrasing, instructional-prevention, and detection-based defenses (Zhang et al., 2025)." — [LLM05](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM05_DataModelPoisoning.md)
- Non-weight artifacts are a vector: "malicious deserialization (e.g., pickle files) and tampering of chat templates, tokenizer configs, LoRA/PEFT adapters, and quantization artifacts, any of which can execute harmful code or alter model behavior when loaded." — [LLM05](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM05_DataModelPoisoning.md)

**LLM06:2026 Unbounded Consumption**
- The defining property is **cost asymmetry**: "Attackers can trigger disproportionately expensive computation at negligible cost to themselves, whether through crafted prompts, stolen credentials, or manipulated workflows." — [LLM06](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM06_UnboundedConsumption.md)
- **"Traditional request-rate limiting alone is no longer sufficient. Effective defense demands token-aware cost controls, hard spending caps, agent-level circuit breakers, and continuous cost-attribution monitoring."** — [LLM06](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM06_UnboundedConsumption.md)
- **Denial of Wallet (DoW)** is a named risk class distinct from DoS: "attackers exploit the cost-per-use model of cloud-based AI services, leading to unsustainable financial burdens on the provider and risking financial ruin." — [LLM06](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM06_UnboundedConsumption.md)
- Reasoning models create a new surface input validation cannot see: "Attackers craft short, benign-looking prompts that result in resource exhaustion by forcing extended-thinking models into prolonged or non-terminating reasoning loops, consuming massive thinking-token budgets while bypassing input-size filters (Li et al., 2025). **Because these prompts are small and appear legitimate, standard input validation provides no protection.**" — [LLM06](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM06_UnboundedConsumption.md)
- Concrete controls: "Move beyond requests per second to enforce limits on **tokens-per-minute, tokens-per-day, and estimated cost per request. Use pre-flight token estimation to reject requests before inference begins.**" — [LLM06](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM06_UnboundedConsumption.md)
- Named vulnerable serving frameworks: "vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, SGLang, Triton, Ollama" — via "unsafe deserialization flaws, special-token injection, and injected chat templates." — [LLM06](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM06_UnboundedConsumption.md)

**LLM07:2026 Misinformation**
- The definition is decision-centric, not accuracy-centric: output that is "incorrect, incomplete, unsupported, or misleading" and "appears credible enough to influence a human decision, an automated workflow, or an agent action. **The core risk is that the incorrect output is trusted and acted upon.**" — [LLM07](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM07_Misinformation.md)
- **Overreliance is now folded into this entry** (it was its own 2023 item): "Humans and systems often treat fluent, confident, or well-structured outputs as authoritative. **In agentic architectures, this overreliance is frequently embedded in system design.**" — [LLM07](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM07_Misinformation.md)
- The named control pattern is **claim-check-act**: "Separate generation from execution and verify claims before acting." Paired with "Use Verification Signals (Not Just Confidence): Incorporate groundedness and consistency checks." — [LLM07](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM07_Misinformation.md)
- Omission is a first-class failure with a structural fix: "Detect and Prevent Omission Failures: **Require structured outputs with mandatory fields.**" The worked example is clinical: "A clinical summary omits a drug contraindication, and a clinician acts on the incomplete recommendation." — [LLM07](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM07_Misinformation.md)
- Cross-agent propagation scenario worth quoting to a finance client: "A retrieval agent reports a customer as identity-verified when it is not, and a downstream payment agent trusts that state and releases funds." — [LLM07](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM07_Misinformation.md)

**LLM08:2026 Hidden Context Exposure** *(renamed/re-scoped from 2025's System Prompt Leakage)*
- Broadened definition: "the unauthorized extraction, inference, or reconstruction of hidden, non-user-facing system instructions or **operational context**" — which "typically includes the system prompt, developer instructions, retrieved policy text (from RAG knowledge bases, configuration stores, or user-profile services), the schemas of tools and functions the application exposes to the model." — [LLM08](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM08_HiddenContextExposure.md)
- The design rule: "**Practitioners should design under the assumption that hidden context is discoverable and that any contents of the context should not be considered a secret.**" Hidden context must not be "solely relied upon as a security boundary for authorization, privilege separation, policy enforcement, or content filtering." — [LLM08](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM08_HiddenContextExposure.md)
- **The severity ladder — the most directly reusable governance artifact in the whole list:** *informational* (no secrets, no security-relevant logic, no reliance on confidentiality) → *medium* (internal rules, filtering criteria, role descriptions, or workflow logic that meaningfully aids an attacker but does not gate critical decisions) → *high* (embedded credentials or tokens, or reliance on hidden-context secrecy for authorization or content policy) → *critical* (disclosure chains to RCE, broad data exfiltration, or privilege escalation in a connected system). — [LLM08](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM08_HiddenContextExposure.md)
- It is explicitly an **amplifier** entry: disclosed rules enable targeted LLM01; embedded credentials become LLM02; revealed tool schemas expand LLM03; leaked formatting rules facilitate LLM10. — [LLM08](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM08_HiddenContextExposure.md)
- Reverse-engineering refusal logic is called out: leakage "reveals the underlying triggers, conditions, and exceptions that led to that decision," letting attackers "craft inputs that intentionally avoid known refusal patterns." — [LLM08](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM08_HiddenContextExposure.md)

**LLM09:2026 Vector and Embedding Weaknesses**
- Scope is any similarity-search system, not just RAG: "vector-backed agent memory, semantic caches, and deduplication pipelines. **Whenever similarity search sits between a data source and the prompt, the embedding layer becomes part of the application's trust boundary.**" — [LLM09](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM09_VectorAndEmbeddingWeaknesses.md)
- The four-way frame worth teaching verbatim: "**poisoning makes the system wrong, inversion makes it leak, jamming makes it silent, and access-control failure makes it indiscriminate.**" — [LLM09](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM09_VectorAndEmbeddingWeaknesses.md)
- These are **not** prompt injection: "They exploit the geometry of the embedding space and the mechanics of similarity search rather than the model's instruction-following behavior. Many succeed even when the retrieved content carries no malicious instructions at all." — [LLM09](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM09_VectorAndEmbeddingWeaknesses.md)
- Cross-tenant leakage happens even with correct tagging: "The attack succeeds even when every document is correctly tagged and every API call authenticated, **because the access-control decision happens after the embedding-space search has run**." Fix: "Enforce tenant scoping inside the index query, not as a post-retrieval filter… A client-supplied scope is a suggestion, not a control." — [LLM09](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM09_VectorAndEmbeddingWeaknesses.md)
- **Embeddings are the source documents, legally:** recovery rates range "from roughly 50–70% of words from sentence embeddings to 92% exact reconstruction of short 32-token inputs with the Vec2Text method (Morris et al., 2023)"; newer zero-shot methods "remain effective against differential-privacy noise added at storage." Therefore "vector-database backups, embeddings shipped to third-party services, and embeddings exposed through misconfigured cloud storage should be treated as equivalent to a leak of the underlying documents." — [LLM09](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM09_VectorAndEmbeddingWeaknesses.md)
- Named CVEs (scoped as compounding, not in-scope): **CVE-2025-64513** (Milvus, forged `sourceID` header bypassing auth, CVSS 9.3) and **CVE-2025-69286** (RAGFlow, predictable token derivation → account takeover, CVSS 9.3). MITRE ATLAS catalogs retrieval poisoning as **AML.T0070 (RAG Poisoning)** under the Persistence tactic. — [LLM09](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM09_VectorAndEmbeddingWeaknesses.md)
- Chunk-level, not document-level, ACLs: "a mostly-public document can contain a confidential paragraph." And on deletion: "Delete embeddings within a bounded time when the source document is deleted, and verify with reconciliation audits." — [LLM09](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM09_VectorAndEmbeddingWeaknesses.md)
- "Vectorless retrieval systems (BM25-only, LLM-native tree navigation) inherit the non-geometric risks but have **no LLM09 attack surface**." — [LLM09](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM09_VectorAndEmbeddingWeaknesses.md)

**LLM10:2026 Improper Output Handling**
- The mental model: "Since LLM-generated content can be controlled by prompt input, this behavior is similar to **providing users indirect access to additional functionality**." Exploitation yields "XSS and CSRF in web browsers as well as SSRF, privilege escalation, or remote code execution on backend systems." — [LLM10](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM10_ImproperOutputHandling.md)
- The one-line control: "**Treat the model as any other user, adopting a zero-trust approach**, and apply proper input validation on responses coming from the model to backend functions." Deferred to existing standards: OWASP ASVS for validation/encoding, parameterized queries, strict CSP. — [LLM10](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM10_ImproperOutputHandling.md)
- Two newer sinks the 2026 edition adds: **terminal/log/IDE control characters** ("ANSI escape sequences, BEL, OSC, backspace, carriage return" → visual spoofing, clipboard hijacking via OSC 52) and **auto-fetching client renderers** ("Markdown images, link previews, iframes" → exfiltration of context data via the image URL's hostname or query string). — [LLM10](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM10_ImproperOutputHandling.md)
- Boundary vs LLM07: "Improper Output Handling concerns **unsafe use** of model output before it is passed downstream, while LLM07:2026 Misinformation concerns output that is **incorrect or misleading**." — [LLM10](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLM10_ImproperOutputHandling.md)

### The framework machinery — why an ID beats an adjective

- Appendix A "consolidates the mappings from the ten OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications (2026) risk entries to **nine external security frameworks and taxonomies**," replacing per-entry mapping sections "in favor of this single, **version-pinned** reference." — [Appendix A: Related Framework Mappings](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/Appendix_A_Related_Framework_Mappings.md)
- The nine, with pinned versions: **ASI 2026 · DSGAI v1.0 (2026-03-17) · MITRE ATLAS v2026.06 · MITRE ATT&CK v19.1 · CWE 4.20 · NIST AI 600-1 v1.0 (July 2024) · NIST AI RMF (AI 100-1) v1.0 (2023) · CSA AICM v1.1 (2026-06-22) · OWASP AIVSS v0.8.** — [Appendix A](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/Appendix_A_Related_Framework_Mappings.md)
- The coverage matrix uses a three-state legend: **●** primary ("a central line of defense or description of the risk"), **○** supporting, **—** no applicable mapping. Every entry maps ● to ASI, ATLAS, CWE, NIST AI 600-1, and CSA AICM; **NIST AI RMF is ● only for LLM04 and LLM07** and **— for LLM10**. — [Appendix A](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/Appendix_A_Related_Framework_Mappings.md)
- **Awareness doc ≠ verification standard.** "The OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications is a *risk-awareness* document… The OWASP AI Security & Privacy Verification Standard (AISVS) is a *verification* standard: it decomposes AI security into testable requirements ('**Verify that** …'), each assigned an assurance Level from L1 (baseline) to L3 (advanced)." The mapping appendix answers one question: *"Given this risk, which AISVS requirements should I verify to know I am defended?"* — [Appendix: Mapping the Top 10 to OWASP AISVS 1.0](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLMZZ_Appendix_AISVS_Mapping.md)
- AISVS 1.0's twelve control chapters: C1 Training Data Integrity & Traceability · C2 Input Validation · C3 Model Lifecycle Management & Change Control · C4 Infrastructure, Configuration & Deployment Security · C5 Access Control & Identity · C6 Supply Chain Security for Models · C7 Model Behavior, Output Control & Safety Assurance · C8 Memory, Embeddings & Vector Database Security · C9 Orchestration & Agentic Security · C10 Model Context Protocol (MCP) Security · C11 Adversarial Robustness · C12 Monitoring, Logging & Anomaly Detection. — [AISVS mapping appendix](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLMZZ_Appendix_AISVS_Mapping.md)
- **The honesty clause to repeat to clients:** "Mappings are directional guidance, **not a compliance crosswalk**: a marked chapter contributes to defending a risk but rarely 'closes' it on its own." — [AISVS mapping appendix](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/LLMZZ_Appendix_AISVS_Mapping.md)
- The release ships a single-diagram threat model — "LLM application architecture and threat model… showing data flows and trust boundaries across the **application, model, and data planes**." — [Appendix B: LLM Application Architecture and Threat Modeling](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/main/2026/final/Appendix_B_LLM_Application_Architecture_and_Threat_Modeling.md)

### The governance counterpart — NIST AI 600-1

- NIST AI 600-1 is "a cross-sectoral profile of and companion resource for the AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) for Generative AI," issued pursuant to **EO 14110**, approved by the NIST Editorial Review Board on **2024-07-25**. The AI RMF itself "was released in January 2023, and is intended for **voluntary** use." — [NIST AI 600-1, Generative AI Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
- It names 12 risks "unique to or exacerbated by GAI," including the ones that carry this week's client work: **Confabulation** ("the production of confidently stated but erroneous or false content… by which users may be misled or deceived"), **Data Privacy** ("leakage and unauthorized use, disclosure, or de-anonymization of biometric, health, location, or other personally identifiable information or sensitive data"), **Harmful Bias or Homogenization** ("performance disparities between sub-groups or languages… that result in discrimination"), **Human-AI Configuration** ("automation bias, over-reliance"), and **Value Chain and Component Integration**. — [NIST AI 600-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
- NIST scoped it to evidence, not speculation: "This document focuses on risks for which there is an **existing empirical evidence base** at the time this profile was written; for example, speculative risks that may potentially arise in more advanced, future GAI systems are not considered." — [NIST AI 600-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
- A profile "assists organizations in deciding how to best manage AI risks in a manner that is well-aligned with their goals, considers legal/regulatory requirements and best practices, and reflects risk management priorities" — i.e. the mechanism that turns a technical finding into a prioritized, owned line item. — [NIST AI 600-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)

## Client hook

Both week-8 packets hand the team a **surface ask to roll out an AI feature wider** and a **hidden finding that is genuinely alarming**. This day supplies the framework that lets the team deliver the alarming finding as a tracked, owned, severity-rated line item instead of a warning the board rejects.

### Healthcare — Northlake Regional (Week 8: Security / Governance / Responsible AI)

**Client ask:** *"The board wants the headline AI feature: automatic visit-summaries for every patient. Turn it on for everyone."*
**Hidden problem:** the `ai-summary` config sends the **full encounter record (name, DOB, MRN, notes) to a cloud LLM vendor with no BAA** (D13, twist #5), and the contractor's "de-identified analytics export" **drops only the `name` column** while shipping ZIP, dates, MRN, and device IDs (twist #14).

**What this day answers, specifically:**

1. **It gives the finding an ID and a severity the board can act on.** "PHI is going to a vendor with no BAA" becomes **LLM02:2026 Sensitive Information Disclosure** — an entry that names **HIPAA** and **GDPR** among its applicable regimes by name, and whose Tier-1 mitigations are exactly the two things missing here: *"Minimize context: send only task-required fields to external providers. Disable auto-context (`customer_360`, full-record append) unless justified per template"* and *"technically enforce no-train/no-retain rather than policy text alone."* The full-encounter-record send is a textbook auto-context violation.
2. **It names the vendor-governance gap as its own tracked risk.** The no-BAA vendor contract maps to **LLM04:2026 Supply Chain**, risk #7 *"Unclear T&Cs and Data Privacy Policies"* — "Unclear terms and conditions and data privacy policies of model operators can lead to sensitive application data being used for model training and subsequent exposure" — with mitigation #1 requiring suppliers be vetted "including T&Cs and privacy policies" and re-assessed "on changes in their security posture or T&Cs." That is the memo's spine: the BAA is not a legal afterthought, it is the LLM04 control.
3. **It supplies the argument that "de-identified" is not a state you can assert.** LLM02's persistence failure — "once data influences weights, embeddings, or adapters it stays extractable after source deletion" — and its **aggregation** rule ("aggregation across individually-permitted sources… is a disclosure when policy prohibits the synthesized conclusion") are precisely why ZIP + dates + MRN + device IDs is re-identifiable. **LLM09** hardens the point for any vector path: embeddings invert at "50–70% of words… to 92% exact reconstruction of short 32-token inputs," so "vector-database backups, embeddings shipped to third-party services… should be treated as equivalent to a leak of the underlying documents."
4. **It justifies the recommendation gate as a design pattern, not caution.** LLM03's **graduated enforcement** ladder (audit / warn / block / escalate — recoverable actions auto-approve, irreversible ones route to human review) is the shape of the "turn it on for everyone" gate. Sending PHI outside the boundary is irreversible; it escalates.
5. **It gives the redactor a place in a standard.** The Safe-Harbor scrub is not an ad-hoc script — it is LLM02 Tier-1 control #5 ("*Sanitize with classifiers, not regex alone… because regex fails on encoded and cross-lingual output*") and AISVS chapter **C7 Model Behavior, Output Control & Safety Assurance** plus **C5 Access Control**, verifiable at an assurance level. That is what makes the unit-tested redactor an auditable control rather than a contractor's preference.
6. **Cross-framework translation for a client that already has a NIST posture.** LLM02 maps ● to **NIST AI 600-1**, whose **Data Privacy** risk is defined as "leakage and unauthorized use, disclosure, or **de-anonymization** of biometric, **health**, location, or other personally identifiable information." The de-identification failure has a NIST name, not just an OWASP one.

### Finance — Meridian (Week 8: Responsible AI / model governance / fair lending)

**Client ask:** *"The board loves the AI scorer. Roll it out wider and make a nice page about how advanced it is."*
**Hidden problem:** adverse-action reasons cluster on **two generic strings regardless of the real driver** (D10, twist #2); no model documentation, no fair-lending disparity check, no monitoring of denial-reason accuracy. CFPB Circular 2023-03 = no AI exemption.

**What this day answers, specifically:**

1. **It names the generic-reason-code defect as a security-framework risk, not just a compliance one.** **LLM07:2026 Misinformation** defines the failure as output that is "incorrect, incomplete, unsupported, or misleading" and "appears credible enough to influence a human decision" — "the core risk is that the incorrect output is trusted and acted upon." Risk #1 is literally *"Unsupported or False Decision Support: Incorrect or unsupported information influences business, legal, healthcare, **financial**, or operational decisions."* An adverse-action reason that does not reflect the model's real driver is a false representation driving a legally consequential decision.
2. **It supplies the structural fix for the two-generic-strings defect.** LLM07 mitigation #6: *"Detect and Prevent Omission Failures: **Require structured outputs with mandatory fields**"* and #1 *"Ground Claims Before Action: Require outputs to be grounded in authoritative and current sources."* That is the reason-code → specific-consumer-reason mapping, stated as a control: the reason must be grounded in the model's actual drivers, and the schema must make a generic fallback impossible.
3. **It makes "roll it out wider" the risk, not the reward.** The 2026 preface's own logic — Misinformation rose 09 → 07 because "the incident record placed it near the top, the widest gap in the direction that actually hurts" — is the on-the-record argument that scaling a model whose outputs drive decisions scales the exposure. And LLM07 maps **●** to **NIST AI RMF** (one of only two entries that do), which is the governance language a bank's model-risk function already speaks.
4. **It gives the model card a framework home.** LLM04 is blunt that documentation and provenance are different things — "**Model Cards document a model but do not prove its origin**" — so the deliverable's model card is the LLM04/AISVS **C3 Model Lifecycle Management & Change Control** artifact, and the team should say what it does and does not establish rather than let the board read it as assurance.
5. **It frames the monitoring spec as a named control.** LLM07 mitigation #8: *"Monitor and Test for Misinformation: **Log claims, evidence, and outcomes**, and test adversarial scenarios,"* and #10 *"Adversarial Evaluation and Continuous Testing."* Denial-reason accuracy monitoring and the disparity check are instances of that, which lets the team scope them as recurring controls with owners rather than a one-off audit.
6. **It supplies the bias vocabulary that maps to fair lending.** NIST AI 600-1's **Harmful Bias or Homogenization** is defined to include "performance disparities between sub-groups or languages, possibly due to non-representative training data, **that result in discrimination**" — the approval-rate-by-ZIP pattern, in the client's regulator-adjacent language. Pair with **Human-AI Configuration** ("automation bias, over-reliance") for why a human "reviewing" the score is not itself a control.
7. **It gives Priya a boring artifact to take to a board.** "LLM07:2026 Misinformation — high — owner: model governance — mitigation: grounded reason-code mapping + denial-reason accuracy monitor — verify: AISVS C7" is a risk-register row. "The AI scorer is an ECOA liability" is a headline. The framework's whole function is converting the second into the first without losing the finding.
