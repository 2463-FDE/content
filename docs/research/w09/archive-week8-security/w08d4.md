### Currency warnings — teach these explicitly

1. **The two CFPB circulars every model cites are withdrawn.** A model writing from memory will cite **Circular 2022-03** ("complex algorithms") and **Circular 2023-03** ("sample forms") as live CFPB authority. Both appear by name in *Interpretive Rules, Policy Statements, and Advisory Opinions; Withdrawal*, **90 FR 20084 (May 12, 2025)** — items 14 and 7 of section III. The Bureau says the withdrawn guidance "should not be enforced or otherwise relied upon by the Bureau while this review is ongoing," and that it "does not intend to prioritize the enforcement of such guidance." **The statute and regulation are untouched** — cite 12 CFR 1002.9(b)(2) and Official Interpretations comments 9(b)(2)-2/-4/-5.
2. **Site state ≠ legal state.** consumerfinance.gov stripped the **2023-03** body to an "Archived content" stub, but **2022-03 still renders in full with no banner** — even though both are in the same withdrawal list. "The page still loads" proves nothing.
3. **AI RMF 1.0 is under revision** and the Playbook URL has moved. Pin the version you cite.
4. **hhs.gov is unreachable to scrapers** (Access Denied to Firecrawl, HTTP 403 to WebFetch). Every claim below about HIPAA de-identification and BAAs is therefore sourced to **eCFR primary text** or to **NIST SP 800-188's quotation of HHS guidance** — not to hhs.gov. This is the better citation anyway; teach students to reach for eCFR over the guidance page.
5. **Federal Reserve SR 11-7 (Model Risk Management) now 404s** at `federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1107.htm` and `sr1107a1.pdf`, and OCC Bulletin 2011-12 404s. **Not cited here.** If a student's model-governance memo leans on SR 11-7, they must re-verify the URL first.

## Claims

### 1. The boundary question, made concrete: a data map is a named artifact with a row spec

- A data map "shows the data processing environment and includes the components through which data are being processed or with which individuals are interacting, the owners or operators of the components, and discrete data actions and the specific data elements being processed" — and "a data map is an important artifact in privacy risk assessment." — [NIST Privacy Framework 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/CSWP/NIST.CSWP.01162020.pdf)
- The map "can be overlaid on existing system/product/service design artifacts for convenience and ease of communication between organizational components" — i.e. it is not a separate compliance deliverable, it is an annotation on the architecture diagram you already have. — [NIST Privacy Framework 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/CSWP/NIST.CSWP.01162020.pdf)
- The Inventory-and-Mapping category is a literal column spec: **ID.IM-P1** systems inventoried; **ID.IM-P2** "Owners or operators (e.g., the organization or third parties such as service providers, partners, customers, and developers) and their roles"; **ID.IM-P3** categories of individuals; **ID.IM-P4** data actions; **ID.IM-P5** purposes; **ID.IM-P6** "Data elements within the data actions are inventoried"; **ID.IM-P7** "The data processing environment is identified (e.g., geographic location, internal, cloud, third parties)"; **ID.IM-P8** "Data processing is mapped, illustrating the data actions and associated data elements … including components; roles of the component owners/operators; and interactions of individuals or third parties." — [NIST Privacy Framework 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/CSWP/NIST.CSWP.01162020.pdf)
- The paired governance family is **ID.DE-P, Data Processing Ecosystem Risk Management**: ID.DE-P2 "Data processing ecosystem parties (e.g., service providers, customers, partners, product manufacturers, application developers) are identified, prioritized, and assessed using a privacy risk assessment process"; ID.DE-P3 "Contracts with data processing ecosystem parties are used to implement appropriate measures." — [NIST Privacy Framework 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/CSWP/NIST.CSWP.01162020.pdf)
- **HIPAA makes the API call itself the regulated event.** "*Disclosure* means the release, transfer, provision of access to, or divulging in any manner of information outside the entity holding the information." No volume threshold, no "it's just an integration" carve-out. — [45 CFR 160.103 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-160/subpart-A/section-160.103)
- **Teaching synthesis (label as synthesis).** Minimum row for the memo's table: `source system | data elements | destination | operator | region | purpose | agreement covering this hop | retention at destination | evidence link`. Any row with an empty "agreement" cell is a finding, not a backlog item. ID.IM-P6 + ID.IM-P7 are what turn "we send the encounter to the model" into "we send `name, DOB, MRN, notes` to vendor X, us-east, under no agreement."

### 2. Third-party AI risk is a named category in the AI RMF, not an afterthought

- "Risk can emerge both from third-party data, software or hardware itself and how it is used. Risk metrics or methodologies used by the organization developing the AI system may not align with the risk metrics or methodologies uses by the organization deploying or operating the system. Also, the organization developing the AI system may not be transparent about the risk metrics or methodologies it used." — [NIST AI 100-1, AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)
- **GOVERN 6**: "Policies and procedures are in place to address AI risks and benefits arising from third-party software and data and other supply chain issues." **GOVERN 6.1**: policies "that address AI risks associated with third-party entities." **GOVERN 6.2**: "Contingency processes are in place to handle failures or incidents in third-party data or AI systems deemed to be high-risk." — [NIST AI 100-1, AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)
- The RMF's definition of the actor you're contracting with: "Third-party entities include providers, developers, vendors, and evaluators of data, algorithms, models, and/or systems and related services… The technologies acquired from third-party entities may be complex or opaque, and **risk tolerances may not align** with the deploying or operating organization." — [NIST AI 100-1, AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)

### 3. Under HIPAA, the written agreement *is* the permission

- "A covered entity may disclose protected health information to a business associate and may allow a business associate to create, receive, maintain, or transmit protected health information on its behalf, **if the covered entity obtains satisfactory assurance** that the business associate will appropriately safeguard the information." — [45 CFR 164.502(e)(1)(i) (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.502)
- "The satisfactory assurances … **must be documented through a written contract** or other written agreement or arrangement with the business associate that meets the applicable requirements of § 164.504(e)." — [45 CFR 164.502(e)(2) (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.502)
- A cloud LLM vendor doing summarization is squarely inside the definition: a business associate is a person who, on behalf of a covered entity, "creates, receives, maintains, or transmits protected health information for a function or activity regulated by this subchapter, including … **data analysis, processing or administration**." — [45 CFR 160.103 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-160/subpart-A/section-160.103)
- The obligation chains downstream: business associate "includes … a **subcontractor** that creates, receives, maintains, or transmits protected health information on behalf of the business associate." — [45 CFR 160.103 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-160/subpart-A/section-160.103)
- **What the contract must contain** (164.504(e)(2)): establish permitted and required uses/disclosures; the BA will "not use or further disclose the information other than as permitted or required by the contract or as required by law"; "use appropriate safeguards and comply, where applicable, with subpart C … with respect to electronic protected health information"; "report to the covered entity any use or disclosure … not provided for by its contract of which it becomes aware, **including breaches of unsecured protected health information as required by § 164.410**"; ensure subcontractors "agree to the same restrictions and conditions"; make PHI available under §164.524 (access), §164.526 (amendment), and "the information required to provide an **accounting of disclosures** in accordance with § 164.528." — [45 CFR 164.504(e)(2) (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.504)
- **Signing is not the end of the duty.** "A covered entity is not in compliance … if the covered entity knew of a pattern of activity or practice of the business associate that constituted a material breach or violation of the business associate's obligation under the contract … unless the covered entity took reasonable steps to cure the breach or end the violation, and, if such steps were unsuccessful, **terminated the contract**." — [45 CFR 164.504(e)(1)(ii) (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.504)
- **Teaching synthesis (label as synthesis).** A standard SaaS ToS satisfies none of 164.504(e)(2). "We have a contract with the vendor" and "we have a BAA with the vendor" are different findings, and the memo must say which one is true.

### 4. Vendor agreement scope is **per-feature**, and default-no-training is not permission

- Anthropic's BAA is scoped surface by surface. Covered: Chat, Projects, Artifacts, Voice, Web Search, Research, Skills. File creation & code execution: covered "**excluding network access and use of external websites**." Explicitly **not** covered for third-party sends: "**MCPs / Connectors**," "Enterprise Search / 'Ask Your Org'," "Claude in Chrome" — each annotated "*available to use but sending data to 3rd parties via this feature isn't covered under Anthropic's BAA.*" Cowork and beta surfaces (Claude in Office, Claude Design): not covered. — [Business Associate Agreements (BAA) for Commercial Customers — Anthropic](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/8114513-business-associate-agreements-baa-for-commercial-customers)
- Coverage requires an affirmative configuration act, not a plan tier: "the Primary Owner of the organization must activate HIPAA compliance in the HIPAA-ready Claude Enterprise organization settings under 'Data and privacy' and accept Anthropic's BAA. **Standard Claude Enterprise plans do not include BAA coverage without action from a Primary Owner.**" — [Business Associate Agreements (BAA) for Commercial Customers — Anthropic](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/8114513-business-associate-agreements-baa-for-commercial-customers)
- Retention configuration and BAA coverage are coupled: Claude Code CLI is "**Only covered under the BAA with ZDR enabled** … Without ZDR enabled, this feature is available to use but is not covered under Anthropic's BAA." Conversely, "**Covered Models** require 30-day data retention and aren't available with zero data retention (ZDR) enabled" — so some model choices and some BAA-covered surfaces are mutually exclusive. — [Business Associate Agreements (BAA) for Commercial Customers — Anthropic](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/8114513-business-associate-agreements-baa-for-commercial-customers)
- The BAA also "only covers the single organization that accepted it," and "customers of Anthropic's HIPAA-ready services are subject to certain configuration requirements and limitations on what features/integrations are available (e.g., the BAA would not apply to use of the **web search** functionality)" — note this article and the BAA page disagree on web search, which is itself the lesson: **read the agreement's own coverage table, dated, at the time you write the memo.** — [Zero data retention scope — Anthropic](https://privacy.anthropic.com/en/articles/8956058-i-have-a-hipaa-compliance-requirement-can-i-use-anthropic-s-services)
- Controller/processor roles are contractual and explicit: "the customer is the 'Controller' of the data submitted by its Users… **Anthropic acts as a 'Processor'** … Anthropic only processes the data as instructed by the customer in order to provide the Claude service," and "Anthropic does not use the data you share when using our commercial products to train our models, unless you choose to participate in our Development Partner Program." — [Does Anthropic Act as a Data Processor or Controller? — Anthropic](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/9267385-does-anthropic-act-as-a-data-processor-or-controller)
- OpenAI's equivalents: "By default, data from ChatGPT Business, ChatGPT Enterprise, ChatGPT for Healthcare, ChatGPT Edu, ChatGPT for Teachers, and the API Platform (after March 1, 2023) **isn't used for training our models**, unless you have explicitly opted in." — [Enterprise privacy at OpenAI](https://openai.com/enterprise-privacy/)
- "OpenAI may securely retain API inputs and outputs for **up to 30 days** to provide the services and to identify abuse. After 30 days, API inputs and outputs are removed from our systems… You can also request **zero data retention (ZDR)** for eligible endpoints if you have a qualifying use-case." — [Enterprise privacy at OpenAI](https://openai.com/enterprise-privacy/)
- "We are able to sign **Business Associate Agreements (BAA)** in support of customers' compliance with the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA). Please reach out if you require a BAA." — [Enterprise privacy at OpenAI](https://openai.com/enterprise-privacy/)
- **Teaching synthesis (label as synthesis).** Default-no-training is a *use* control and 30-day retention is a *retention* control. Neither is a *permission to disclose*. Under 164.502(e) the permission comes only from the executed BAA — and the BAA's coverage table decides which code paths are legal. A data-flow row must therefore name the **feature/surface**, not just the vendor: "Claude, covered" is a false statement; "Claude Enterprise Chat with HIPAA activated — covered; the MCP connector hop — not covered" is a finding.

### 5. In banking, the duty does not transfer to the vendor either

- "A banking organization's use of third parties **does not diminish its responsibility** to meet these requirements to the same extent as if its activities were performed by the banking organization in-house," including "fair lending laws and prohibitions against unfair, deceptive or abusive acts or practices." — [Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management (88 FR 37920)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/06/09/2023-12340/interagency-guidance-on-third-party-relationships-risk-management)
- The guidance is **final as of June 6, 2023**, is issued jointly by the Board, FDIC and OCC, and "replaces each agency's existing general guidance on this topic." — [Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management (88 FR 37920)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/06/09/2023-12340/interagency-guidance-on-third-party-relationships-risk-management)
- Contract clauses it names — a ready-made checklist for the vendor memo: the org's "ability to access its data in an appropriate and timely manner"; "access to, or use of, the third-party's data and any supporting documentation"; how data "may be shared with **regulators** in a timely manner as part of the supervisory process"; "**whether the third party is permitted to resell, assign, or permit access to customer data, or the banking organization's data, metadata, and systems, to other entities**"; notification of "compliance lapses, enforcement actions, regulatory proceedings"; notification of "use of subcontractors"; and "specification of the type and frequency of reports." — [Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management (88 FR 37920)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/06/09/2023-12340/interagency-guidance-on-third-party-relationships-risk-management)
- **Right to audit**: "a contract includes provisions for periodic, independent audits of the third party **and its relevant subcontractors** … the types and frequency of audit reports the banking organization is entitled to receive (for example, SOC reports, Payment Card Industry (PCI) compliance reports…)" and "may also reserve the banking organization's right to conduct its own audits." — [Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management (88 FR 37920)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/06/09/2023-12340/interagency-guidance-on-third-party-relationships-risk-management)
- Also: SLA performance measures should not "incentivize imprudent performance or behavior, such as encouraging processing volume or speed without regard for accuracy, compliance requirements, or adverse effects on the banking organization or customers." — [Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management (88 FR 37920)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/06/09/2023-12340/interagency-guidance-on-third-party-relationships-risk-management)
- **State the caveat out loud:** "supervisory guidance does not have the force and effect of law and does not impose any new requirements on banking organizations." — [Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management (88 FR 37920)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/06/09/2023-12340/interagency-guidance-on-third-party-relationships-risk-management)

### 6. De-identification: exactly two lawful methods, and Safe Harbor is 18 categories plus a knowledge test

- Method 1, **expert determination**: a qualified person "determines that the risk is very small that the information could be used, alone or in combination with other reasonably available information, by an anticipated recipient to identify an individual," **and** "documents the methods and results of the analysis that justify such determination." — [45 CFR 164.514(b)(1) (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.514)
- Method 2, **Safe Harbor**: remove the listed identifiers "of the individual **or of relatives, employers, or household members** of the individual." — [45 CFR 164.514(b)(2)(i) (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.514)
- The list, verbatim (A)–(R): **(A)** Names; **(B)** "All geographic subdivisions smaller than a State, including street address, city, county, precinct, zip code, and their equivalent geocodes, except for the initial three digits of a zip code if … the geographic unit formed by combining all zip codes with the same three initial digits contains more than 20,000 people; and the initial three digits of a zip code for all such geographic units containing 20,000 or fewer people is changed to 000"; **(C)** "All elements of dates (except year) for dates directly related to an individual, including birth date, admission date, discharge date, date of death; and all ages over 89 and all elements of dates (including year) indicative of such age, except that such ages and elements may be aggregated into a single category of age 90 or older"; **(D)** Telephone numbers; **(E)** Fax numbers; **(F)** Electronic mail addresses; **(G)** Social security numbers; **(H)** Medical record numbers; **(I)** Health plan beneficiary numbers; **(J)** Account numbers; **(K)** Certificate/license numbers; **(L)** Vehicle identifiers and serial numbers, including license plate numbers; **(M)** Device identifiers and serial numbers; **(N)** Web URLs; **(O)** IP address numbers; **(P)** Biometric identifiers, including finger and voice prints; **(Q)** Full face photographic images and any comparable images; **(R)** "Any other unique identifying number, characteristic, or code." — [45 CFR 164.514(b)(2)(i)(A)–(R) (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.514)
- The second, non-mechanical condition: "the covered entity **does not have actual knowledge** that the information could be used alone or in combination with other information to identify an individual who is a subject of the information." — [45 CFR 164.514(b)(2)(ii) (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.514)
- **The pseudonym trap.** A re-identification code is allowed only if it "is not derived from or related to information about the individual and is not otherwise capable of being translated so as to identify the individual," and the covered entity "does not use or disclose the code … for any other purpose, and **does not disclose the mechanism for re-identification**." A hash of the MRN is *derived from* information about the individual and fails (c)(1). — [45 CFR 164.514(c) (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.514)
- And re-identifying is itself regulated: "the disclosure of a code or other means of record identification designed to enable coded or otherwise de-identified information to be re-identified constitutes disclosure of protected health information; and if de-identified information is re-identified, a covered entity may use or disclose such re-identified information only as permitted or required by this subpart." — [45 CFR 164.502(d)(2) (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.502)
- **Teaching synthesis (label as synthesis).** Dropping only the `name` column clears exactly one of eighteen categories. ZIP is (B) and clears only at 3 digits subject to the 20,000-population rule; full dates never clear (C); MRN is (H); device IDs are (M). The contractor's "de-identified analytics export" is PHI, and the engineer who knows MRN maps to a patient satisfies the *actual knowledge* trigger in (b)(2)(ii) personally.

### 7. De-identification is a tested artifact — NIST SP 800-188 supplies the test plan

- The single most quotable line for the deliverable: "**not all tools that merely mask personal information provide sufficient functionality for performing de-identification.**" — [NIST SP 800-188, De-Identifying Government Datasets (Sept 2023)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-188.pdf)
- Safe Harbor's classification: "A **prescriptive** de-identification standard specifies an algorithmic procedure that – if followed – results in data that are de-identified to a previously established benchmark. This approach focuses on the **process** of de-identification rather than on the specific properties of the de-identified data. The Safe Harbor method of the HIPAA Privacy Rule is an example." Its intent is "to provide covered entities with a simple method to determine if the information is adequately de-identified." — [NIST SP 800-188 (Sept 2023)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-188.pdf)
- Why getting it right matters: "Once de-identified, the dataset is **no longer subject to HIPAA privacy, security, and breach notification regulations.**" — [NIST SP 800-188 (Sept 2023)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-188.pdf)
- Residual risk is doctrine, not opinion — 800-188 quoting HHS guidance: "Both methods, even when properly applied, yield de-identified data that retains some risk of identification. **Although the risk is very small, it is not zero**, and there is a possibility that de-identified data could be linked back to the identity of the patient to which it corresponds." — [NIST SP 800-188 (Sept 2023)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-188.pdf)
- The validation checklist (§4.6.2) — this is the redactor's test plan: examine outputs "to make sure that no identifying information is unintentionally included in **file data or metadata**"; check k-anonymity "if such a standard is desired"; "**critically evaluat[e] all default assumptions used by software that performs data modification or modeling**"; and conduct a "**motivated intruder test** to see if reasonably competent outside individuals can perform re-identification using publicly available datasets, commercially available datasets, or even private datasets that might be available to certain data intruders." Results should be reported "for both vulnerable populations and the general population." — [NIST SP 800-188 (Sept 2023)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-188.pdf)
- Its own honesty about the ceiling: "These approaches do not provide provable guarantees on the protection offered by formal privacy techniques, but they may be useful as part of an overall agency risk assessment." — [NIST SP 800-188 (Sept 2023)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-188.pdf)
- Governance wrapper: agencies "can create a **Disclosure Review Board** to oversee the process of de-identification," "adopt a de-identification standard with **measurable performance levels**," and "perform **re-identification studies** to gauge the risk associated with de-identification." — [NIST SP 800-188 (Sept 2023)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-188.pdf)
- The contrast case worth one sentence: differential privacy "makes no distinction between which data are considered identifying and which are not. Nor does using differential privacy require classifying data as direct identifiers, quasi-identifiers, or non-identifying values… [it assumes] that all values in a confidential record must be protected." — [NIST SP 800-188 (Sept 2023)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-188.pdf)
- **Teaching synthesis (label as synthesis).** Test-suite shape for the W8 redactor: one positive + one negative case per identifier category (A)–(R); identifiers embedded in **narrative free text**, not just columns; format variants (`MRN 00123` / `mrn-00123` / `#123`; `3/4/26` / `2026-03-04` / "last Tuesday"; age `91` → `90 or older`); the ZIP3 ≤20,000 → `000` rule; **metadata** cases (filename, EXIF, JSON sibling keys). Run entirely **offline** on fixtures — never validate a redactor by spamming a live LLM (this is also the week's quota guardrail). Report recall per category; a recall miss on a direct identifier is release-blocking.

### 8. Model documentation is a file in the repo, and transparency ≠ fairness

- The original proposal: "released models be accompanied by documentation detailing their performance characteristics," providing "**benchmarked evaluation in a variety of conditions**, such as across different cultural, demographic, or phenotypic groups … and intersectional groups … that are relevant to the intended application domains," and disclosing "the **context in which models are intended to be used**, details of the performance evaluation procedures, and other relevant information." — [Model Cards for Model Reporting (arXiv:1810.03993)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993)
- Stated purpose, which is exactly the W8 "say no" lever: "In order to **clarify the intended use cases** of machine learning models and **minimize their usage in contexts for which they are not well suited**…" — [Model Cards for Model Reporting (arXiv:1810.03993)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993)
- Companion artifact for the data side: — [Datasheets for Datasets (arXiv:1803.09010)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09010)
- The operational form that makes it an engineering artifact rather than a slide: "model cards are simple Markdown files with additional metadata… You can find a model card as the **`README.md` file in any model repo**," with "a **YAML section at the top** that contains metadata about the model" (`language`, `tags`, `license`, `datasets`, `base_model`, `library_name`). — [Model Cards · Hugging Face Hub docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/model-cards)
- What the card should describe, per the Hub spec: "the model; its **intended uses & potential limitations, including biases and ethical considerations** as detailed in Mitchell, 2018; the training params and experimental info; which datasets were used to train your model; the model's evaluation results." — [Model Cards · Hugging Face Hub docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/model-cards)
- Where documentation sits in the risk framework: "Trustworthy AI depends upon accountability. **Accountability presupposes transparency.**" Transparency's scope "spans from design decisions and training data to model training, the structure of the model, its intended use cases, and how and when deployment, post-deployment, or end user decisions were made and by whom." — [NIST AI 100-1, AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)
- The caveat that kills the "responsible AI page" instinct: "**A transparent system is not necessarily an accurate, privacy-enhanced, secure, or fair system.**" — [NIST AI 100-1, AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)

### 9. Adverse-action reasons: the circulars are withdrawn; the regulation is not

- **The withdrawal, verbatim from the list**: item 7, "Consumer Financial Protection Circular 2023-03: Adverse action notification requirements and the proper use of the CFPB's sample forms provided in Regulation B, 89 FR 27361"; item 14, "Consumer Financial Protection Circular 2022-03: Adverse action notification requirements in connection with credit decisions based on complex algorithms, 87 FR 35864." — [Interpretive Rules, Policy Statements, and Advisory Opinions; Withdrawal, 90 FR 20084 (May 12, 2025)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/05/12/2025-08286/withdrawal-of-guidance-documents)
- The Bureau's posture: "the Bureau has determined that the guidance identified in section III **should not be enforced or otherwise relied upon by the Bureau** while this review is ongoing," and "the Bureau does not intend to prioritize the enforcement of such guidance against parties that do not conform to the guidance during the pendency of any withdrawal." But: "**Such withdrawal is not necessarily final.** The Bureau intends to continue reviewing all guidance documents to determine whether they should ultimately be retained." — [90 FR 20084 (May 12, 2025)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/05/12/2025-08286/withdrawal-of-guidance-documents)
- The 2023-03 page now shows only: "Archived content / [View this page as it was originally published.] / Content has been archived / Page last modified Apr. 11, 2025." — [CFPB Circular 2023-03 (archived page)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/circulars/circular-2023-03-adverse-action-notification-requirements-and-the-proper-use-of-the-cfpbs-sample-forms-provided-in-regulation-b/)
- **What is still binding — the regulation.** "The statement of reasons for adverse action … must be specific and indicate the principal reason(s) for the adverse action. Statements that the adverse action was based on the creditor's internal standards or policies **or that the applicant … failed to achieve a qualifying score on the creditor's credit scoring system are insufficient.**" — [12 CFR 1002.9(b)(2) (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1002/section-1002.9)
- **Comment 9(b)(2)-2**: "The specific reasons disclosed … must **relate to and accurately describe the factors actually considered or scored** by a creditor." — [Regulation B Official Interpretations, Supplement I to Part 1002 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1002/appendix-Supplement%20I%20to%20Part%201002)
- **Comment 9(b)(2)-4**: "If a creditor bases the denial or other adverse action on a credit scoring system, the reasons disclosed must relate **only to those factors actually scored in the system**. Moreover, **no factor that was a principal reason for adverse action may be excluded from disclosure.** The creditor must disclose the actual reasons for denial (for example, 'age of automobile') even if the relationship of that factor to predicting creditworthiness may not be clear to the applicant." — [Regulation B Official Interpretations (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1002/appendix-Supplement%20I%20to%20Part%201002)
- **Comment 9(b)(2)-5** gives an implementable selection algorithm: "The regulation does not require that any one method be used… One method is to identify the factors for which the applicant's score fell furthest below the average score for each of those factors achieved by applicants whose total score was at or slightly above the minimum passing score. Another method is to identify the factors for which the applicant's score fell furthest below the average score … achieved by all applicants… Any other method that produces results substantially similar to either of these methods is also acceptable." — [Regulation B Official Interpretations (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1002/appendix-Supplement%20I%20to%20Part%201002)
- **Comment 9(b)(2)-1**: "The regulation does not mandate that a specific number of reasons be disclosed, but disclosure of more than four reasons is not likely to be helpful to the applicant." **Comment 9(b)(2)-8**: for an automatic-denial factor, "the creditor must disclose that specific factor." **Comment 9(b)(2)-3**: "A creditor need not describe how or why a factor adversely affected an applicant." — [Regulation B Official Interpretations (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1002/appendix-Supplement%20I%20to%20Part%201002)
- **Background only — cite as withdrawn, dated 2022.** Circular 2022-03's reasoning is still the clearest articulation of the principle: "ECOA and Regulation B do not permit creditors to use complex algorithms when doing so means they cannot provide the specific and accurate reasons for adverse actions"; "A creditor's **lack of understanding of its own methods** is therefore not a cognizable defense against liability"; and its footnote 1: "While some creditors may rely upon various post-hoc explanation methods, such explanations approximate models and creditors **must still be able to validate the accuracy of those approximations**, which may not be possible with less interpretable models." — [CFPB Circular 2022-03 (withdrawn 2025-05-12)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/circulars/circular-2022-03-adverse-action-notification-requirements-in-connection-with-credit-decisions-based-on-complex-algorithms/)
- **Teaching synthesis (label as synthesis).** Two generic reason strings emitted regardless of the model's real drivers violate 1002.9(b)(2) and comments -2/-4 on their own terms — no circular required. Comment -5 gives the reason-selection algorithm the mapping should implement and be tested against; comment -1 caps useful output at ~4 reasons; comment -8 forces automatic-denial factors to surface as themselves. The FDE lesson: **when the guidance you learned gets withdrawn, walk one level down to the regulation and the finding survives.**

### 10. The delivery move: refuse with a gate, not with a "no"

- The framework's own hook for this: **GOVERN 6.2** — "**Contingency processes are in place to handle failures or incidents in third-party data or AI systems deemed to be high-risk.**" A widening condition that produces no artifact cannot be audited later. — [NIST AI 100-1, AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)
- The vendor side makes at least one condition concrete and third-party-verifiable: BAA coverage requires a Primary Owner to activate HIPAA compliance and accept the BAA, and some surfaces are covered only with ZDR enabled — so "covered surface now, uncovered surface after the agreement is amended" is a dated, checkable gate. — [Business Associate Agreements (BAA) for Commercial Customers — Anthropic](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/8114513-business-associate-agreements-baa-for-commercial-customers)
- Anchor the *gate* to statute/regulation and the *timeline* to a review date, because guidance moves: the CFPB's own withdrawal notice says "such withdrawal is not necessarily final," and the interagency guidance says "supervisory guidance does not have the force and effect of law." — [90 FR 20084](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/05/12/2025-08286/withdrawal-of-guidance-documents) / [88 FR 37920](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/06/09/2023-12340/interagency-guidance-on-third-party-relationships-risk-management)
- **Teaching synthesis (label as synthesis).** The pattern, five levers for narrowing: **scope** (one unit, not all), **data** (de-identified / minimum-necessary payload, not the full record), **autonomy** (draft-for-review, not auto-publish), **surface** (only the vendor features the agreement covers), **volume** (capped pilot with logged decisions). Then two conditions, each with *owner + artifact + date*, and a stated failure branch ("if a condition slips, the gate holds"). Sentence template: *"We can ship \<gated version\> by \<date\>. It widens to \<full version\> when (1) \<condition A: artifact + owner + date\> and (2) \<condition B: artifact + owner + date\>."* Good conditions are binary and evidence-backed ("executed BAA covering surface X, countersigned, on file"); bad conditions are "once legal is comfortable."

## Client hook

Both W8 packets run the **same trap**: the board asks for a *wider rollout* of an AI feature, and the honest answer is "not as specified — here's the gated version and the two conditions." Neither ask can be met by a marketing page or a config flag. This day supplies the four artifacts that make the refusal constructive: the **data-flow/trust-boundary map**, the **vendor-agreement scope memo**, the **tested de-identification/mapping artifact**, and the **model documentation**.

### Healthcare (Meridian-style clinic) — W8 surface ask: *"The board wants the headline AI feature: automatic visit-summaries for every patient. Turn it on for everyone."*

What this day's topic answers, item by item:

| Thing in the W8 materials | What today's sources settle |
|---|---|
| `ai-summary` config sends the **full encounter record (name, DOB, MRN, notes)** to a cloud LLM vendor | 45 CFR 160.103's definition of *disclosure* — "provision of access to, or divulging in any manner … outside the entity" — makes the POST body a disclosure of PHI. The data-flow map (NIST PF ID.IM-P6/P7) is the artifact that states which elements go where, to which operator, in which region. |
| Vendor contract is a **standard ToS, no BA addendum** | 164.502(e)(1)(i)+(e)(2): disclosure to a business associate is permitted **only** with documented satisfactory assurances; 164.504(e)(2) lists the terms a ToS does not contain (breach reporting under 164.410, subcontractor flow-down, access/amendment/accounting support). The vendor is a BA under 160.103(1)(i) ("data analysis, processing or administration"). **This is the memo's headline finding: no BAA = impermissible disclosure, not a paperwork gap.** |
| Contractor's "de-identified analytics export" **drops `name` only**, ships ZIP, dates, MRN, device IDs | 164.514(b)(2)(i)(A)–(R) verbatim: `name` is (A); ZIP is (B) and only clears at 3 digits under the 20,000-population rule; full dates are (C); MRN is (H); device IDs are (M). Plus (b)(2)(ii) **actual knowledge**. NIST SP 800-188 supplies the test plan (metadata inspection, tool-default challenge, motivated-intruder test) that turns the scrub into a **unit-tested redactor** rather than a claim — and the line "not all tools that merely mask personal information provide sufficient functionality for performing de-identification" is the memo's epigraph. |
| A tempting "just hash the MRN" fix | 164.514(c)(1): the code must "not [be] derived from or related to information about the individual." A hash of the MRN fails. 164.502(d)(2) adds that disclosing the re-identification mechanism is itself a disclosure of PHI. |
| The **recommendation gate** the deliverable asks for | The gated-rollout pattern: e.g. *summaries on de-identified input for one clinic, draft-for-clinician-review, on the BAA-covered vendor surface only* — widening on (1) executed BAA covering the specific surfaces named in the vendor's own coverage table, and (2) redactor passing its per-identifier recall bar plus a motivated-intruder test, each with owner and date. Anthropic's per-feature BAA table is the concrete example that "the vendor is HIPAA-ready" is not a row you can write; "surface X covered, MCP hop not covered" is. |

Deliverable mapping: **Safe-Harbor scrub + unit-tested redactor** ← claims §6/§7; **data-flow + BAA/vendor-governance memo** ← claims §1/§3/§4; **recommendation gate** ← claim §10.

### Finance (Meridian-style lender) — W8 surface ask: *"The board loves the AI scorer. Roll it out wider and make a nice page about how advanced it is."*

| Thing in the W8 materials | What today's sources settle |
|---|---|
| Decision logs show **adverse-action reasons clustering on two generic strings** regardless of the real driver | 12 CFR 1002.9(b)(2) (must be "specific and indicate the principal reason(s)"; qualifying-score statements "are insufficient") + comment 9(b)(2)-2 ("must relate to and accurately describe the factors **actually considered or scored**") + comment 9(b)(2)-4 (only scored factors; **no principal-reason factor may be excluded**). Comment 9(b)(2)-5 gives the **reason-selection algorithm** to implement; comment 9(b)(2)-1 caps at ~4. **This is the reason-code → specific-consumer-reason mapping's acceptance criteria.** |
| The packet's premise cites **CFPB Circular 2023-03** as the "no AI exemption" authority | ⚠️ **Both 2022-03 and 2023-03 were withdrawn at 90 FR 20084 (2025-05-12).** The finding survives — because it never depended on the circular. This is the single best in-week demonstration of the FDE habit: *walk down to the regulation.* Cite 1002.9(b)(2) + comments; cite 2022-03 as dated background for its reasoning ("a creditor's lack of understanding of its own methods is … not a cognizable defense"; post-hoc explanations "approximate models and creditors must still be able to validate the accuracy of those approximations"). |
| **No model documentation** | Mitchell et al. (arXiv:1810.03993) for content — intended use, out-of-scope use, **disaggregated** evaluation by relevant groups, evaluation procedure; Hugging Face Hub spec for form — a `README.md` with YAML front-matter that lives in the repo, versions with the model, and reviews in a PR. |
| "Make a nice page about how advanced it is" | NIST AI RMF 1.0: "Accountability presupposes transparency" — **and** "A transparent system is not necessarily an accurate, privacy-enhanced, secure, or fair system." A marketing page is not a governance artifact; the model card + monitoring spec are. |
| Rolling the scorer **wider** | GOVERN 6/6.1/6.2 (third-party AI risk + contingency processes) and the interagency third-party guidance's "use of third parties does not diminish its responsibility … including fair lending laws." The gated version: keep the current portfolio segment, add the corrected reason mapping and the disparity/reason-accuracy monitor, and widen on (1) reason-accuracy validated against the model's real drivers at a stated threshold, (2) the fair-lending monitoring spec running with a named owner and a first report date. |
| Vendor/data hops in the scoring pipeline | The interagency contract-clause checklist — data access, regulator access, resale/assignment of "data, **metadata**, and systems," subcontractor notification, **right to audit the third party and its relevant subcontractors** — is the vendor section of the governance package. Caveat that this guidance "does not have the force and effect of law." |

Deliverable mapping: **reason-code → specific-consumer-reason mapping** ← claim §9; **model card** ← claim §8; **fair-lending monitoring spec** ← claims §8/§10 (the monitor is the artifact that a widening condition points at); **vendor/third-party section** ← claim §5.

### The shared trap, stated for the authoring agent

Both packets ask for **wider**. Both hide a defect that gets *multiplied* by width — an unsignaled PHI disclosure in healthcare, an ECOA exposure per applicant in finance. And in both, the surface ask ("turn it on for everyone" / "make a nice page") is a **communication artifact**, while the real deliverable is a **governance artifact**. The refusal only lands if it arrives with a dated, narrower thing that ships and two conditions that are checkable by someone other than the engineer who wrote them.
