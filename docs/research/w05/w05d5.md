## Review evidence

A defensible graph model review asks:

1. **Question fit:** which client decisions and actions does the model support?
2. **Identity/integrity:** which stable keys and constraints prevent accidental duplicates?
3. **Semantics:** are labels, relationship types/direction, time, and provenance explicit?
4. **Retrieval quality:** does each question use an appropriate retriever and bounded traversal?
5. **Grounding:** can claims be traced to source evidence and graph paths, including conflicting/stale evidence?
6. **Operations:** what are the latency, fan-out, context-size, ownership, correction, and cost risks?

Text-to-Cypher review needs a representative question set, expected properties/paths, read-only execution scope, query validation/limits, and answer-level checks. Exact-string query matching is too brittle when semantically equivalent Cypher exists; review should focus on safety and result correctness.

The executive readout leads with client outcome, decision, evidence, and remaining risk. The simulated technical-team session then shows the model, one representative query/retrieval trace, a failure or abstention case, and the ADR/deferred-decision register. No external client communication is part of the lesson.
