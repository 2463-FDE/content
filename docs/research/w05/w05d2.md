## Source-authoritative scope

Tuesday covers variable-length paths and traversals; indexes and constraints; data-import pipelines; business rules and standards; compliance as a governance layer; ADR lock/defer; AI-native user stories and acceptance criteria; and a graph-schema workshop.

## Verified claims

1. Cypher quantified path patterns and quantifiers express repeated traversal. Unbounded or weakly selective traversal can expand the search space; bounds and selective predicates should reflect the client question.
2. Search-performance indexes improve eligible lookups. Constraints enforce declared data rules. They have different jobs; an index is not a uniqueness rule.
3. Neo4j supports `LOAD CSV` for CSV import. Idempotent imports require deliberate keys and constraints. `MERGE` should target stable identifying patterns rather than a large mutable pattern.
4. Business rules can be represented as graph data when they need provenance, ownership, versioning, or traversal. Runtime authorization and legal interpretation must remain in their enforceable/owned layers; storing a rule node does not enforce the rule.
5. Schema decisions that affect identity, retention, or downstream interfaces should be **locked** in a dated ADR once evidence is sufficient. Unresolved choices should be **deferred** with an owner, evidence gap, and flip condition—not silently guessed.
6. An AI-native story states the human outcome, machine action, evidence used, and human control. Acceptance criteria should include grounded success, ambiguity, denial, and stale-evidence cases.
