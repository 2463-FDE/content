## Claims

### Discovery starts with stakeholders, not features
- **Definition:** a stakeholder interview is "a conversation with a person who has a vested interested in a project with a goal of gathering insights to drive the project's success." NN/g's recommended coverage is four areas: **success metrics, priorities, history/expertise, and process/workflow** — i.e., "what does success look like?" before "what should it do?" [Stakeholder Interviews 101 — Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/stakeholder-interviews/)
- **Probing technique:** deepen answers with open-ended follow-ups — "Tell me more about that?", "Can you give me an example?", "Why is that important to you?" Common failure modes: interviewing too late, interrupting, closed-ended questions. Findings "should help inform all aspect of your project: UX research, ideation, priorities, timeline, resources, and stakeholder-engagement plans." [Stakeholder Interviews 101 — Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/stakeholder-interviews/)
- **Why interviewers miss things (and where AI helps):** elicitation interviews suffer from "lack of familiarity with the domain, excessive cognitive load, and information overload." Shen, Singhal & Breaux (CMU) show GPT-4o follow-up questions are "no worse than the human-authored questions with respect to clarity, relevancy, and informativeness" — and when generation is *guided by a taxonomy of common interviewer mistakes*, LLM questions outperform human ones. Teaching point: a mistake/coverage framework beats freeform "ask good questions." [Requirements Elicitation Follow-Up Question Generation — arXiv 2507.02858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02858)

### Ambiguity is the enemy — and it measurably breaks AI implementation
- **The core problem (verbatim):** "System requirements are usually written in unconstrained natural language (NL), which is inherently imprecise... requirements problems propagate to lower levels," creating "unnecessary volatility and risk, impacting programme schedule and cost." [EARS — Alistair Mavin (official guide)](https://alistairmavin.com/ears/)
- **The standard's bar:** ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018 (active, supersedes 2011) defines what a well-formed requirement is — the widely-taught characteristic set includes **necessary, unambiguous, complete, singular, feasible, verifiable, traceable**; "unambiguous" means interpretable in only one way, and verifiability is enhanced when the requirement is measurable. [ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148-2018 — IEEE Standards](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/29148/6937/)
- **2025 empirical proof for the AI era:** "ambiguity of NL can harm software quality, as unclear problem descriptions may lead to incorrect program generation." SpecFix repairs ambiguous descriptions *before* generation (contrastive specification inference); modifying 43.58% of benchmark descriptions raised Pass@1 by 30.9% on modified problems, and repairs transfer across models (+10.48% when a description repaired for one model is reused by others). Spec precision is now a *code-quality* lever, not just a documentation nicety. [Automated Repair of Ambiguous Problem Descriptions for LLM-Based Code Generation — arXiv 2505.07270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07270)

### EARS: the lightweight syntax for unambiguous requirements
- **Generic structure (verbatim):** "While <optional pre-condition>, when <optional trigger>, the <system name> shall <system response>". Five patterns, clauses always in the same temporal order:
  1. **Ubiquitous** — `The <system name> shall <system response>`
  2. **Event-driven** — `When <trigger>, the <system name> shall <system response>`
  3. **State-driven** — `While <precondition(s)>, the <system name> shall <system response>`
  4. **Unwanted behaviour** — `If <trigger>, then the <system name> shall <system response>`
  5. **Optional feature** — `Where <feature is included>, the <system name> shall <system response>`
  plus **Complex** (combined clauses). [EARS — Alistair Mavin (official guide)](https://alistairmavin.com/ears/)
- **Adoption case:** "EARS is used worldwide by large and small organisations" — Airbus, Bosch, NASA, Rolls-Royce; it's "lightweight, there is little training overhead, no specialist tool is necessary," and it's "effective for requirements authors whose first language is not English." [EARS — Alistair Mavin (official guide)](https://alistairmavin.com/ears/)
- **EARS in current AI tooling:** Kiro's `requirements.md` "captures user stories, acceptance criteria... in structured notation" using EARS, e.g. "WHEN a user submits a form with invalid data THE SYSTEM SHALL display validation errors next to the relevant fields." Specs are the three-file chain `requirements.md → design.md → tasks.md`. [Specs — Kiro Docs](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/) · [Feature Specs — Kiro Docs](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/feature-specs/)

### Normative language: MUST / SHOULD / MAY
- **RFC 2119 definitions (verbatim):** MUST/SHALL = "an absolute requirement of the specification"; SHOULD = "there may exist valid reasons in particular circumstances to ignore a particular item, but the full implications must be understood and carefully weighed"; MAY = "truly optional." And the discipline clause: imperatives "MUST only be used where it is actually required for interoperation or to limit behavior which has potential for causing harm" — not to impose implementation preferences. [RFC 2119 — Key words for use in RFCs](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119)

### Acceptance criteria as executable examples (Gherkin)
- **Given/When/Then semantics:** `Given` "describe[s] the initial context of the system — the *scene* of the scenario... typically something that happened in the *past*"; `When` is the event/action (avoid implementation details); `Then` is "an *expected* outcome, or result" verified by observable assertions. `And`/`But` chain steps; `Background` extracts shared Givens; `Scenario Outline` + `Examples` parameterize one scenario over a data table. A `Feature` file "must always" open with `Feature:` and a short description. Good scenarios are concrete cases of a business rule in 3–5 steps. [Gherkin Reference — Cucumber Docs](https://cucumber.io/docs/gherkin/reference)
- Teaching bridge: EARS states the rule ("the system shall"); Gherkin instantiates the rule as concrete, testable examples. Same requirement, two altitudes.

### Spec-driven development: the discovery→spec pipeline, productized
- **Workflow (Spec Kit v0.12.3, 2026-07-01):** `/speckit.constitution` ("create or update project governing principles"), `/speckit.specify` ("define what you want to build — requirements and user stories"), `/speckit.clarify` ("clarify underspecified areas — recommended before `/speckit.plan`"), then `/speckit.plan`, `/speckit.tasks`, `/speckit.implement`, with `/speckit.analyze` for "cross-artifact consistency & coverage analysis." Each phase emits a Markdown artifact that feeds the next. [github/spec-kit — GitHub](https://github.com/github/spec-kit) · [Spec Kit Documentation](https://github.github.com/spec-kit/)
- **Ambiguity is handled as a first-class gate:** the docs mandate "run the structured clarification workflow **before** creating a technical plan to reduce rework downstream" — `/speckit.clarify` does "sequential, coverage-based questioning that records answers," and specs carry explicit `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION]` markers rather than silent assumptions. This is the discovery-conversation → precise-spec loop, encoded in tooling. [github/spec-kit — GitHub](https://github.com/github/spec-kit) · [Spec Kit Documentation](https://github.github.com/spec-kit/)
- FDE framing for the day: the same gate applies to human stakeholders — an ambiguity you don't surface in the discovery conversation becomes a `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION]` an agent (or teammate) resolves *by guessing*.

## Concept IDs

Scoped tightly to this day (elicitation + ambiguity + spec synthesis). Later-day topics (implementation planning, task decomposition, estimation) deliberately excluded.

- **`stakeholder-discovery-interviews`** — Structured discovery conversations: the four NN/g coverage areas (success metrics, priorities, history, workflow), open-ended probing ("can you give me an example?"), and the failure modes (too late, closed questions, interrupting).
- **`ambiguity-detection-and-clarification`** — Surfacing what's underspecified: interviewer-mistake taxonomies, coverage-based clarification questioning, `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION]` markers, and the evidence that ambiguity measurably degrades LLM code generation (SpecFix).
- **`ears-requirement-patterns`** — The five EARS templates (ubiquitous / event-driven / state-driven / unwanted-behaviour / optional) + complex combinations; fixed clause order as the anti-ambiguity mechanism.
- **`requirement-quality-characteristics`** — ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148's well-formedness bar (unambiguous, singular, verifiable, traceable...) and RFC 2119 normative keywords (MUST/SHOULD/MAY) used sparingly and precisely.
- **`acceptance-criteria-gherkin`** — Given/When/Then as concrete, testable instantiations of a requirement; Background, Scenario Outline/Examples; rule-vs-example altitude.
- **`spec-artifact-pipeline`** — Discovery→spec as a gated artifact chain: constitution → spec → clarify → (plan/tasks downstream); Kiro's requirements.md/design.md/tasks.md and Spec Kit's clarify-before-plan rule.
