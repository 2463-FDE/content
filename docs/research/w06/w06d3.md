## Version stamps

## Claims and first-party evidence

- MSK manages Kafka infrastructure within its documented service boundary; application topic, schema, consumer, lag, replay, and side-effect behavior remain customer responsibilities — [Amazon MSK overview](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/msk/latest/developerguide/what-is-msk.html).
- LangGraph supports durable stateful workflow execution and replay-sensitive design — [LangGraph durable execution](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/durable-execution).
- CrewAI Flows provide stateful, event-driven workflow structure — [CrewAI Flows](https://docs.crewai.com/en/concepts/flows).
- Chroma collections store and update identified records; application identity/version strategy determines replay behavior — [Chroma collections](https://docs.trychroma.com/docs/collections/collections).
- Neo4j transaction functions, constraints, and idempotent domain operations support retry-aware writes; they do not share a transaction with Kafka or Chroma — [Neo4j Python driver transactions](https://neo4j.com/docs/python-manual/current/transactions/) and [constraints](https://neo4j.com/docs/cypher-manual/current/constraints/).
- Consumer lag, partition skew, and rebalancing are Kafka operating signals; throughput and oldest-record age are needed to interpret consequence — [Apache Kafka operations](https://kafka.apache.org/documentation/#operations).
